# typestill

A digital notebook made to be the bridge between digital notes and real commonplace notebooks and zines. Opinionated but flexible, with simplicity at its core. Built on top of Excalidraw for drawing, on the page itself.

The idea: a notebook of fixed-size lined pages you write on like a simple note editor, in Excalidraw's handwriting font, always on the lines, and draw on with Excalidraw's tools, over and around the writing, as on paper. Writing and drawing are two modes of the same page: in drawing mode the text goes quiet (dimmed or hidden, not clickable) and the page is the canvas, at the page's size, no zoom, no pan. Pages come in two kinds: lined pages for writing, and zine pages for images with a little text. No productivity-app machinery. (Until Phase 8 the drawing surface was one infinite canvas per notebook in a side panel; it stays in the code, unused, see section 8.)

## 1. Product principles

- It should feel like a Moleskine with a pen and a pencil, not like a productivity app.
- Writing and drawing are different activities and get different modes of the same page: the editor for text, Excalidraw for everything drawn, one on top of the other.
- Pages are appended in creation order within their section. Never reordered by hand.
- Pages have no title. They have a date, a section, and content.
- The app is not opinionated about organization. Sections, the notebook's physical divisions, are the only structure.
- Text is on the lines by construction. The user never nudges things to keep a page tidy.
- Everything drawn is plain Excalidraw data. We only build the shell around it.
- Simplicity over features. Each page kind does one thing well: lined pages hold writing, zine pages hold images. Images never go into lined pages.
- The owner holds the data: browser storage plus backup files they keep. No accounts, no backend.
- It behaves like a real notebook in human hands. Things stay tied together, and the user plays by its rules rather than the app quietly untying them.
- The spread is the notebook's true unit. Pages have sides, pairs face each other, covers have insides, and printing a notebook means printing spreads. Decisions about pages keep spreads first-class.

## 2. Product spec

### Notebooks
- One notebook at a time. Opening typestill is opening your current commonplace notebook, where it was left; there is no list to choose from. To work on a different notebook, download this one as a backup and open the other one's backup file: the opened notebook takes the current one's place (after asking, since the current one is only kept by its backup). A first run still makes the first notebook through the welcome dialog, or opens a backup.
- A notebook has a fixed number of pages, like a physical one, chosen when it is made: 64, 96, 128 or 192 (96 to begin with), every count a multiple of 4 because a folded sheet is 4 pages and a booklet prints from whole sheets. Every page exists from the start as a blank slot in its position; nothing is added or deleted, a page is cleared instead. Filling the last page is finishing the notebook: it is complete, ready for the next one. In the notebook box the count can grow by whole sheets; it can shrink only while every page that would go is blank and no section would fall past the end.
- The storage stays multi-notebook underneath (a notebook is a record with its own pages, canvas and files, and the shelf and switcher remain in the code, see section 8); the UI simply shows one. The name is set in notebook settings.
- A notebook has many pages and exactly one canvas.
- Every notebook has a cover: a colour from a small palette, an optional emoji or initial, the name, and an optional subtitle. The cover is what the notebook looks like when closed: a small swatch next to the name in the app bar, and the card on the shelf when the shelf is shown again one day. It is set in notebook settings. Image covers are not planned.

### Layout
- The clean UI (2026-09-19, Phase 11): typestill looks like an indie game's screen, not a web app's: the notebook on the desk and almost nothing else. There is no app bar. The wordmark is a small quiet label in the window's top-left corner. Two small buttons sit in the window's bottom-right corner, one above the other: Preview (the Eye) and Help ("?", also Shift+?), which opens a dialog with every shortcut and a short guide to writing, the quick line and shapes, drawing mode, sections, the overview, preview, settings and backups. Everything else is reached from the page or the keyboard: the notebook box by a double-click in the page's top margin (above the first rule) or bottom margin (below the last rule), search with Cmd/Ctrl+F, the overview with the counter or ⌥M, drawing mode with Shift+Tab, the quick line with Shift. Controls that remain are icon buttons with a hover tooltip showing the label and the shortcut. (Until Phase 11 an app bar held the notebook button, search, the notebook menu, preview and, earlier, the canvas panel toggle.)
- Under the page or spread sits the page bar, as wide as the page or spread. It holds only the centre commands, centred on the page: previous, the page counter, next. The counter is a button showing the open page's section as a coloured dot with the number ("● 3 / 96"); it toggles the overview. Nothing at its ends (Phase 11 took the overview button, the two-columns toggle and the drawing-mode pencil off it: ⌥M, the notebook box's This page group and Shift+Tab do those); the pool toggle for zine pages sits at its right end only while the zine flag is on. There is no new page and no delete page: the notebook has its pages. The bar belongs to the page the way a caption does, so it moves with the page when the desk is resized, and the page is fitted above it.
- The desk is the whole window below the app bar, with the page or spread centred; there is no rail (the notebook's map lives in the notebook box, see below). A rounded side panel on the right, resized from the gap between the two with its width remembered per browser, exists only for the media pool on zine pages (it opens from the page bar there and closes with the page). The canvas panel is gone; drawing happens on the page. The desk is a very light warm grey and the page or spread lies on it with the faintest shadow. ⌥\ toggles the clean layout: the page bar, the corner buttons, the wordmark and, in the overview, the written count and the section names are hidden in place, so nothing around the notebook moves. Per tab, not stored. (It was Shift+? until Phase 11 gave that chord to Help.)
- One box holds the notebook's settings: the notebook box, a dialog opened by a double-click in the page's top or bottom margin (the strip above the first rule or below the last, where nothing is written; on a zine page the same strips of the page). Top to bottom: the Sections group (each section's name, colour and length in sheets, with "+ sheet" and "− sheet"; "Add section", delete), the This page group (the open page's section, read only; its layout, two columns; its kind behind the flag; when it was created; "Clear page…", which asks first), the notebook's own groups (name and cover; paper size, orientation and the number of pages; theme and margin line; the new-page defaults; the app appearance; storage), and a Notebook group with the actions that were the app bar's menu: Download backup, Open backup…, Export PDF, Export page as PNG. Changes apply as they are made; there is no Save. The grid view (below) is not in the box: seeing the notebook and setting it are different things.
- The overview: the notebook flipped through at a glance, spread by spread, toggled with ⌥M, the page bar's overview button or its counter, drawn over the desk and closed the same way, with Escape or a click on a page. See "The overview".
- The text column shows one page, fit to the available space at the paper's aspect ratio. With the panel closed it shows the open page's spread: a left-hand page with the next page on its right, the two touching at the gutter with no gap, as a notebook lies open. The notebook is a sequence of sides: the inside of the front cover, then pages 1 to N, then the inside of the back cover; sides alternate left and right from the inside cover, so odd pages are right-hand pages and the spreads run (inside cover | 1), (2 | 3), (4 | 5) and so on to (N | inside back cover). Every section starts on a sheet boundary, page 4k+1, so a section always opens on a right-hand page with the previous section's last page on the left, and no divider leaf is needed; the section shows in the page bar's counter and the grid view, never on the page. The inside of a cover is a flat page-sized surface in the chrome's colours (white in the light appearance, dark grey in the dark one), with nothing on it and nothing to click. The page of a spread that is not open is dimmed until it is clicked. This is the ground for a second cover, an inside cover and a counter cover later.
- A page has two modes, writing and drawing, switched from the page bar (a pencil, Shift+Tab) and remembered per browser for the session; see Drawing.

### Pages (text)
- Page size is set per notebook: A5, A4 or Letter. Orientation is set per notebook: portrait or landscape.
- A page is lined paper: horizontal rules at a fixed pitch, a top margin, and a light red margin line on the left. Text sits on the rules. The margin line position is a notebook setting (20mm to begin with) that every page follows, as one bound notebook has one margin; it is no longer adjusted per page (the per-page field stays stored, unused, see section 8).
- Writing works like a simple note editor: typing, line breaks, wrapping at the column edge, caret, selection, undo, copy and paste. Plain text only. The font is Excalifont, Excalidraw's handwriting font. Tab inserts four spaces (it never moves the focus away from the page); Shift+Tab stays the drawing-mode toggle.
- Paragraph alignment: a fourth alignment next to left, centre and right, and the opinionated one. A paragraph aligned this way starts at the margin line (the red rule) and, when it runs past the column's edge, its wrapped lines and Shift+Enter lines continue from the page's left edge, in the margin strip, until the paragraph ends; Enter starts the next paragraph at the rule again. So the rule marks where paragraphs begin and the body of a long paragraph fills the whole width, as on a page written by hand. Left, centre and right stay exactly as they were: every one of their lines sits right of the rule (left at the rule, centre in the middle, right at the right edge), never past it. New paragraphs take the paragraph alignment unless the alignment is changed (the owner made it the default on 2026-09-19); Enter keeps the alignment. In the second column, which has no rule, paragraph alignment indents the first line by the same amount as in the first column, so paragraphs look alike across the divider.
- A page holds a fixed number of lines, and each page is its own editor: writing on one never flows to the next. The editor is continuous within the page, as any editor is: any edit or alignment change reflows what is below it, nothing is ever refused. When the text runs past the last rule the page says so, "3 lines past the end", and the lines past the rule stay visible below it until the page's edge; beyond the edge there is no more page, as on paper. Trimming is the user's; the page only tells them. (From Phase 1 to Phase 10 a full page refused input, the hard stop; replaced 2026-09-19 by the continuous page.)
- Optional divider: a light gray vertical line that splits the page into two columns of text. It defaults to the middle of the writable area (from the margin line to the right edge), snaps to 10mm steps when moved, and can be removed. A new page inherits the divider of the page it was created from. "Two columns" is toggled from the page settings popover, next to the page's metadata (number, date).
- The page is a white sheet with a thin border, no shadow. Every page has a side, derived from its position and never stored: odd pages are right-hand pages, even pages left-hand. A right-hand page rounds its right corners and a left-hand page its left corners; the inner edge is square, in single-page view too.
- Basic formatting: bold, italic, text colour from a small palette shared with the canvas (Excalidraw's five stroke colours), highlight, and paragraph alignment (left, centre, right, and the paragraph mode described below). Alignment works like Google Docs: it applies to the whole paragraph, including its wrapped lines and any line breaks inside it, and to every paragraph a selection touches. Enter starts a new paragraph; Shift+Enter breaks a line within the paragraph. A small bar floats over a selection with bold, italic, the five swatches, the highlighter and the four alignments; the shortcuts are Cmd+B, Cmd+I, Cmd+Shift+H and Cmd+Shift+L / E / R, as in Google Docs. Nothing else: no font sizes, lists or links. The text stays on the lines whatever the formatting.
- Highlight: a marker stroke behind a run of text, translucent, so the ink and the rule show through as under a real highlighter. Four tints shared with the canvas (Excalidraw's element background picks: yellow, green, blue, pink), yellow first; each theme maps the tints to its paper, so Dark has its own. The format bar's highlighter button applies the last tint used (yellow to begin with) and toggles it off on a highlighted selection; its swatches pick a tint or none. Cmd+Shift+H does the same as the button. Bold stays as it is: synthetic and faint in Excalifont, real in Plain's typeface and in zine text.
- Pages are neither added nor deleted: the notebook has its pages from the start, numbered by position. A page is cleared instead ("Clear page…" in the notebook box's This page group, asking first), which empties its writing and drawing and leaves the slot in place; an empty page can change kind. Flipping past the last page is not possible: the notebook is full.
- Nothing is printed on the page but what the user puts there: no page number and no date stamp. The page bar says which page is open, and the creation date and time show in the notebook box. Numbering the pages of an export is a later option of the export itself (the stored `showPageNumber` fields wait for it, unused, see section 8). (The page number was inside the page from Phase 2 to Phase 8; the date stamp existed in Phases 2 and 3.)
- Preview: the notebook as it reads. The Eye in the app bar turns the desk into a vertical scroll of the spreads, one below the other at the desk's size, cover to cover (the front cover alone on the right, then (inside cover | 1), (2 | 3) … (N | inside back cover), then the back cover alone on the left), every page exactly as it is, rules, margin line and divider included, since they are the page's layout, only read-only and without editing chrome, its drawing shown as a still. It reads like a published PDF or a Behance project: scroll from the first page to the last without the notebook changing size. Scrolling moves the open page with it (the spread crossing the middle of the view), so the counter follows and leaving preview lands where the reading stopped. Nothing is clickable on the pages; the quick line, double-click and drawing mode are off; the Eye again or Escape returns to the desk. Preview is also what exports render (a page in preview is a page as it prints).

### Pages (zine)
- A second page kind, for images, next to lined pages. A notebook mixes both freely. The kind is chosen when a page is created and can change only while the page is empty.
- A zine page is composed in place from blocks, like a Behance project but small. An empty page shows a plus on hover; clicking it offers a single image, a grid or a text block. Once a block is there, hovering below it offers what can go beneath, and hovering the left or right edge of the media block offers a text block beside it. Nothing is dragged into position.
- At most one media block per page (a single image or a grid of two to four), at most one text block beside it and one below it. A single image with nothing else fills the whole page, page number area included. A text block below reserves rows beneath the media; the taller the text block, the shorter the image, which stays cropped to cover. Two blocks in a row split the width in half.
- Padding is the only layout setting left in page settings: zero (the default) bleeds images to the page edges; otherwise the same inset on every side and the same gap between blocks. Everything about a block (its rows of text, a grid's preset, an image's fit or replacement, removing it) is in the tools that appear when the block is hovered.
- Text blocks use the same editor as lined pages with the same formatting and the same continuous behaviour (text past a block's rows shows below it and the block says so), but no rules and the theme's zine font, a typeface rather than the handwriting of lined pages. A text block's height is its rows of text, default four, changed from its hover tools.
- Grid presets: two side by side, two stacked, two by two. Each cell fills with its image cropped to cover it; a per-image "fit" option letterboxes instead. Empty cells show a placeholder until an image is dropped in.
- Images come from paste, drop or a file picker. They are downscaled on import (long edge 2048px, re-encoded), stored once per notebook by content hash in the same files table the canvas uses, and inlined in backups.
- Zine pages remember a canvas view like lined pages, appear in the rail like any page, face each other in the two-page spread, and export at physical size like lined pages.
- Zine pages are behind a feature flag, so the app can ship with lined pages and the canvas first and zine pages in a later release. With the flag off, nothing makes a new zine page (the New page control makes a lined page outright, the ⌥⇧N shortcut does nothing, page settings show no Kind row); a zine page already in the notebook (from a backup made with the flag on) still opens, edits and exports as ever, with the pool beside it, so no data is hidden or lost. The flag is a build-time setting (`VITE_FEATURE_ZINE`, on in development, off in production builds) with a per-browser override in local storage for looking at either state in either build. It goes away when zine pages ship.

### Media pool
- Every notebook has one media pool: every image in the notebook, whether placed on a zine page or pasted into a page's drawing. One image is one record, by content hash, however many places use it.
- The side panel shows the pool while a zine page is open; it is the panel's only content now that drawing happens on the page.
- Images enter the pool by dropping files on the pool or on a page, by paste, or with a file picker. They are downscaled on import (see section 5). The same file imported twice is one image.
- An image is placed by dragging it from the pool onto a cell of the page's media block, or by selecting a cell and clicking an image. Dropping onto a filled cell replaces the image there. (Dragging an image onto the infinite canvas went dormant with it, section 8.)
- The pool looks like a phone's photo library: square thumbnails edge to edge in a tight grid of three or more columns, newest first, no captions. Only a slim bar sits above it: the peek control, an add button (drop and paste work anywhere on the pool), and the count.
- Usage is a small badge on the thumbnail, always visible: "p. 2", "3 pages", "canvas", or nothing when unused. An image in use cannot be deleted; the user replaces it first. Unused images show a delete control on hover, which asks first and removes the image from the notebook.

### Themes
- A theme is the look of the pages and nothing else: the fonts, the line grid, the rules and margin line, the colours, the zine defaults. The app's chrome is not themed.
- Graphite ink and black bold (2026-09-19): the page's ink is a mechanical-pencil grey, dark enough to read comfortably and clearly not black, and bold text is the full black. Excalifont has one weight and the browser's synthetic bold barely shows, so the weight of a word is said with its darkness, as pressing harder with a pencil does; typefaces with a real bold (Plain's, the zine's) get the weight as well. Coloured text keeps its colour, bold or not. Each theme sets both colours: Ruled and Plain a warm graphite and a warm black, Dark a soft light grey and a near-white.
- Every notebook references one theme. Changing it re-lays out every page at once; text keeps its lines, and the usual rule for text past the last line applies.
- Built-in themes come with the app. "Ruled" is today's look: Excalifont on lines at 7mm with the red margin line. "Plain" is a monospaced typeface with no visible rules and no margin line, on the same invisible grid. Rules can be lines, dots or none; the grid and the hard stop are always there, since they are the constraint, not the decoration.
- "Dark" is the third built-in theme, right after the first two: dark paper, light ink, dimmed rules and margin line, the same five colour picks. Because the pages are themed and the chrome is not, Dark comes with an app appearance setting, light, dark or system, that the chrome and the canvas follow (Excalidraw has its own dark theme). Picking the Dark theme suggests the dark appearance; the two stay separate settings.
- Custom themes come later: made in settings from the same fields, with user-provided font files stored in the notebook, travelling in backups. A notebook template, meaning a theme plus notebook defaults and a page size, is also later.

### Drawing (on the page)
- Every page, lined or zine, carries a drawing: plain Excalidraw elements in the page's own coordinates (scene px from the page's top-left corner, the same millimetre scale as everything on the page). Drawing mode puts an Excalidraw editor over the open page at exactly the page's size and the page's on-screen zoom: no zoom, no pan, the page is the canvas. Strokes keep their physical size whatever the window.
- In drawing mode the writing is locked: the text and the rules stay visible but take no clicks, so drawing over them is easy. Two dimming controls sit with the drawing tools, "Text" and "Rules", each full, dim or hidden; they are ways of seeing while drawing, remembered per browser, never stored with the page and never applied to exports. Highlighting is drawing: a wide translucent stroke over a line of text.
- Tools: selection, pen, line, arrow, rectangle, ellipse, text, eraser, with Excalidraw's own stroke, fill, width and opacity choices. Images come in by paste and become image elements in the drawing, stored in the notebook's files table. Hand, frames, embeds, the laser and the rest are hidden. What is drawn may run past the page edge; the page clips it.
- In writing mode the drawing shows as a still picture over the text, and the text is editable again. There is no over-or-under choice: each drawn element has its own opacity in Excalidraw, which is how a drawing sits back (the stored `drawingLayer` is unused, see section 8). Thumbnails, the preview and exports show writing and drawing together, always at full opacity.
- Quick line: without leaving writing, holding Shift arms the open page (a crosshair; the text, cells and handles take no clicks) and dragging draws one line that follows the pointer freely (no angle snapping, the owner's call after trying the 15° steps: a hand-drawn underline is not a ruled one). Letting go adds the line to the page's drawing as an ordinary Excalidraw line element, in the stroke colour, width and roughness last used in drawing mode (Excalidraw's defaults to begin with), and the still shows it at once; releasing Shift disarms. A Shift+click without a drag does nothing, and the caret stays where it was: the line is drawn without taking the keyboard from the editor. While Shift is held the mouse cannot extend a text selection (Shift+arrows still do); the moment Shift is up the editor is exactly as before. It works on the open page in writing mode only, lined or zine, not in preview, not on the other page of a spread, not in drawing mode (where Excalidraw's own Shift applies), and not while the overview or the notebook box is open. Undo: Cmd/Ctrl+Z undoes the page's last action, whichever it was: a quick line goes away, a text edit is the editor's undo, and repeated presses walk back through both in the order they happened; there is no redo for a quick line. While Shift is held, a right click (a two-finger click on a trackpad) undoes the quick line just placed, and a right click during a drag, while the line is only being previewed, cancels it and places nothing; the context menu does not appear while the page is armed. A line can still be removed in drawing mode like any element.
- Quick shapes: holding Shift is really a quick drawing mode, and the line is its default element. With Shift held, tapping Option (Alt) cycles the element, round robin: line, rectangle, ellipse, arrow, line again. The choice lasts while Shift is held and the next drag draws it; releasing Shift returns to the line. A small indicator on the armed page, at its top-right corner, shows the current element's icon and name, so what the next drag will draw is never a guess. The drag's start and end are a rectangle's or ellipse's corners; an arrow runs start to end with its head at the end. Shapes take the remembered stroke, no fill, and undo, cancel and right click exactly as the line does. Nothing here touches typing: Option alone types nothing, and the letter keys stay the editor's.
- Into drawing and back without the shortcut: in writing mode, a double-click on a drawn element (on its stroke, or anywhere on a drawn text or image) enters drawing mode with that element selected, ready to move, resize or delete; a double-click on writing that has no element under it selects a word as ever, even inside a drawn box, since only the outline counts. In drawing mode, Escape with nothing selected and the selection tool active leaves drawing mode (Excalidraw's own Escape deselects and cancels a tool first, so it takes one more press). Shift+Tab and the pencil stay.
- Drawing mode is for the open page only; the other page of a spread shows its drawing still. Entering drawing mode on a zine page closes the pool panel.
- The infinite canvas of Phases 1 to 5 stays in the data and in backups, unused (section 8): nothing drawn there is lost, and nothing is moved onto pages by itself.

### Sections
- A section is a division of the notebook, as the tabbed dividers of a physical notebook make: a journal, work notes and reflections can share one commonplace notebook and still be found at once. Every page belongs to exactly one section; every notebook has at least one. (Sections replaced tags, which were one label per page or none.)
- A section is a cut: it starts at a page and runs to the next cut or the end. Cuts fall on sheet boundaries only (page 1, 5, 9, …), so a section is a whole number of sheets, at least 4 pages, and can be printed as its own booklet; the first section starts at page 1 and cannot be deleted. A page's section follows from the cuts; nothing is stored on the page.
- Sections have a name and a colour from the cover palette, and a length in sheets. They are edited in the notebook box's Sections group only: name, colour, and the length with "+ sheet" and "− sheet" (a column of 4 pages appended to or taken from the section's end, shifting the cuts after it; the last section is the notebook's remainder and keeps at least one sheet, so nothing can grow past the end); "Add section" cuts the last sheet off the last section as a new section at the end (off, with a tooltip, while the last section has one sheet); deleting a section merges it into the one before, after a confirm naming its pages; the first section cannot be deleted.
- Page numbers are positions; a section is a contiguous run of sheets. Moving a cut moves pages between sections without moving any page.
- The section colour colours its name in the overview and the dot in the page bar's counter.
- Each section remembers the page last opened in it, so its name in the overview returns there; a section not yet visited opens at its first page.
- Experiment, the tabs (2026-09-18): a real notebook's dividers have index tabs that stick out past the page edge, staggered top to bottom so every one shows when the book is closed, and there are only as many as the page's height fits. Here every section's tab is visible all the time on the edges of the page or spread: each section has a fixed slot down the edge, in section order, and its tab sits in that slot on the left edge when its divider is in the left stack (the sections up to and including the open page's) and on the right edge when it is in the right stack (the sections after). The tab is the section's colour with its name written along it; the open page's section sticks out a little further. Clicking a tab opens the section where it was left (as the map's tabs do); hovering shows the name. The limit is one tab per 38mm of page height: five on A5 portrait, seven on A4 and Letter, three on A5 landscape; adding a section past it is off with a tooltip saying so, and a paper size that would not fit the notebook's sections is refused with a message. The divider leaf keeps its place in the side sequence (a section still opens on a right-hand page, with a blank back before it when needed) but is drawn as paper carrying its own tab in its slot, front and back, instead of a coloured slab. Behind the `tabs` flag (on by default; `typestill.features.tabs` in local storage overrides): off, the slabs and the tab along the back's edge return.

### The overview
- The notebook flipped through at a glance: a scrollable column of its spreads, one below the other, cover to cover, drawn over the desk. First the front cover alone on the right (the cover swatch at page size: colour, emoji or initial, name, subtitle), then (inside front cover | 1), (2 | 3), (4 | 5) and so on to (N | inside back cover), then the back cover alone on the left. Each page is its thumbnail; a blank page is the page's layout in miniature, its rules and margin line drawn by CSS at the thumbnail's scale (no image), faded so the written pages stand out; a page with content whose thumbnail has not been rendered yet shows paper until the overview renders it in the background, one page at a time, while it is open. Cover insides are the same plain surfaces as on the desk.
- Sections appear where they begin: the section's name, in its colour, beside the spread whose right-hand page is the section's first page, like a margin note. Nothing else marks sections, and nothing marks the open page. Clicking a section's name opens the section where it was left.
- Clicking a page opens it and closes the overview. Hovering a page shows its label ("Page 7 · Work"). It opens scrolled to the open page's spread.
- A line at the top says how far the notebook has come: "63 of 96 pages written" (a page counts as written when its fill is above 0), which is what the fixed count is for.
- Toggled with ⌥M, the page bar's overview button or its counter; closed the same way, with Escape, or by opening a page. It replaces the grid view of squares, which read as a control panel rather than a notebook.
- The grid view (2026-09-18, built, refined, and replaced the same day by the overview): one square per page in columns of four, shaded by fill, with section names as month labels. Too abstract for a notebook; the spreads say the same thing in the notebook's own terms. Fill stays, for the count.
- Experiment, the edge (2026-09-18, tried and discarded the same day): the sections and pages on the desk as the notebook's fore-edge, one thin line per page coloured by section. Built behind a flag, looked at, reverted; the map stays in the box. Recorded in the decisions log.

### Search
- Full-text search across all pages, their writing and the text drawn on them. Results jump to the page. Cmd/Ctrl+F opens it (the browser's find is taken over on the page; Cmd+K is no longer bound), as a floating box at the top centre of the desk; Escape closes it.

### Storage
- Browser storage (IndexedDB), autosave on every change.
- Download the notebook as a backup file. Open a backup file to restore: the opened notebook becomes the current one, after asking, and the notebook it replaces stays only in whatever backup was downloaded of it.

### Export
- PDF of the whole notebook, every page in position, blank ones included, writing and drawing together; the count is a multiple of 4, so the file is ready for imposition into booklets. For now exports leave out the rules, margin line and divider (the page's `bare` rendering); the print options, later, will offer that as a choice, with the page's layout kept as the other.
- PNG of the current page, same treatment.

### Platform
- Desktop browsers only.
- Personal use first, but built so it can be shipped to others.
- Keep the door open for sync and collaboration later. Do not build them now.

## 3. Data model

```
Notebook {
  id: string
  name: string
  createdAt: number
  lastOpenedAt: number
  lastPageId: string | null            // the page the notebook opens at
  cover: { color: string, emoji: string | null, subtitle: string | null }
  themeId: string                      // a built-in theme, or later a custom one
  pageSize: "A5" | "A4" | "Letter"
  orientation: "portrait" | "landscape"
  defaults: {
    showPageNumber: boolean
    margin: number                     // margin line offset in mm for new pages
    divider: number | null
  }
  size: number                         // pages, a multiple of 4: 64, 96, 128, 192 to choose from, more by whole sheets
  sections: Section[]                  // in order of start; at least one, the first starting at 0
}

Section {
  id: string
  name: string
  color: string                        // from the cover palette
  start: number                        // 0-based position of its first page, a multiple of 4; runs to the next start or the end
  lastPageId: string | null            // where the section was left; its name in the overview returns there
}

Page {
  id: string
  notebookId: string
  createdAt: number                    // when the slot was made
  position: number                     // 0-based slot in the notebook: the page order and the number minus one
  kind: "lined" | "zine"
  fill: number                         // 0 to 1, how full the page is, updated on save; counts as written in the overview above 0
  showPageNumber: boolean
  margin: number                       // margin line offset in mm
  columns: Column[]                    // lined pages: one or two columns
  zine?: Zine                          // zine pages
  divider: number | null               // mm from the left edge, null for one column
  drawing: ExcalidrawElement[]         // the page's drawing, in page coordinates; empty for none
  drawingLayer: "over" | "under"       // where the still drawing paints in writing mode
  canvasView: { scrollX, scrollY, zoom } | null   // dormant with the infinite canvas (section 8)
}

Column {
  text: string                         // plain text mirror of doc: search, and what a backup reader without the editor can use
  doc: EditorDocument                  // ProseMirror JSON: paragraphs with an align attr; text with bold, italic, colour and highlight marks; hard breaks
}                                      // the highlight mark's attr is a tint id: "yellow" | "green" | "blue" | "pink"

Zine {
  padding: number                      // mm; 0 = images bleed to the page edges; also the gap between blocks
  rows: ZineRow[]                      // top to bottom; an empty page has none
}

ZineRow { blocks: ZineBlock[] }        // one block, or two side by side sharing the width equally

ZineBlock =
  | { kind: "image", image: { fileId: string, fit: "cover" | "contain" } | null }
  | { kind: "grid", layout: "row" | "column" | "square", images: ({ fileId, fit } | null)[] }   // 2 side by side, 2 stacked, 2 by 2
  | { kind: "text", column: Column, rows: number }                                              // rows of text at the zine line height
                                       // the shape is general; the app allows one media block, one text beside it, one text below

Theme {
  id: string
  name: string
  lined: {
    font: Font
    pitchMm: number                    // rule pitch, and the text line height
    firstRuleMm: number
    bottomMm: number                   // no rule closer than this to the bottom edge
    rules: "lines" | "dots" | "none"
    marginLine: boolean
    defaultMarginMm: number
    textInsetMm: number
    rightInsetMm: number
  }
  zine: { font: Font, defaultPaddingMm: number, defaultTextRows: number }
  colours: { paper: string, ink: string, boldInk: string, rule: string, margin: string, divider: string }   // ink is graphite, boldInk the full black
  page: { border: boolean, cornerMm: number }
}

Font {
  family: string
  source: "bundled" | "file"           // a font shipped with the app, or a file in the notebook
  fileId?: string
  lineHeight: number                   // unitless; the font size is the pitch divided by it
}                                      // the baseline offset is measured at runtime, never stored

Canvas {                               // the infinite canvas of Phases 1 to 5: kept, dormant (section 8)
  notebookId: string                   // one per notebook
  gridEnabled: boolean
  elements: ExcalidrawElement[]        // untouched Excalidraw data
}

NotebookFile { notebookId, id, data: BinaryFileData }   // images, referenced by zine pages, page drawings and the dormant canvas
```

Notes:
- Pages, the canvas and files are separate records so autosave writes only what changed. The shelf reads notebook metadata only.
- A page's `drawing` (and the dormant canvas's `elements`) is valid Excalidraw data. It can be exported as a normal `.excalidraw` file at any time, and future sync/collab can reuse Excalidraw's own reconciliation instead of a custom one.
- Backup file = notebook, pages, canvas and files as one JSON document, images base64 inside; or, once the notebook has images, a zip with the same JSON as `notebook.json` (file entries without their data) and each image as `files/<id>.<ext>`. Both open the same way. Format version 12 (version 12 makes the paragraph alignment the default, and left paragraphs of older files, until then the only default, become paragraph-aligned on read; version 11 only marks that a paragraph's `align` may be "paragraph", the fourth alignment; version 10 gives the notebook its `size` and its sections their `start`, gives pages `position` and `fill` and drops `sectionId`, older files converted by `slotsFromSections`; version 9 replaces `tags` and `tagId` with `sections` and `sectionId`, converted from older files by `sectionsFromTags`; version 8 adds each page's `drawing` and `drawingLayer`, empty and "over" in older files; version 7 only marks that documents may carry the highlight mark; nothing else is converted); version 1 files (plain-string columns) are converted on open, each line break becoming a paragraph boundary, files from before version 3 get the default cover, pages from before version 4 are lined, notebooks from before version 5 use the Ruled theme, and zine pages from before version 6 are converted to rows of blocks with their date stamp fields dropped. The IndexedDB schema has the same upgrades (Dexie versions 2 to 13).
- Page thumbnails are cached separately in IndexedDB (a `thumbnails` table, Dexie version 6) and are not part of the file.
- Version 6 of the backup format and Dexie version 7 (Phase 4) removed the date stamp fields and store zine pages as rows of blocks: the old media block becomes the first row, with the text beside it as that row's second block on the side it had; the text below becomes a second row with the same rows of text; a page with no images and no writing becomes an empty page. Nothing is lost (`src/page/zineLegacy.ts`). The date stamp's removal and the zine change made one migration rather than two.
- Built-in themes are code, not records. Custom themes (later) are records under the notebook and go into the backup with it.

## 5. How the tricky parts get solved

**Text on the lines.** One editor per column, in Excalifont, with the line height equal to the rule pitch and the top placed so the first baseline sits on the first rule; paragraphs have zero margin, so every baseline lands on a rule whatever the formatting. Wrapping at the column edge and everything else (caret, selection, input methods) is the browser's; ProseMirror keeps the document and the DOM in step. A contenteditable rather than a text area so text can later wrap around images floated in the column. The continuous page (2026-09-19, replacing the hard stop of Phases 1 to 10): no transaction is refused. The capacity plugin's `filterTransaction` goes; what stays is the measurement, since the page still needs to know its lines: the mirror renders the document after each change (or the column reads its own laid-out height, which is the same DOM) and reports the lines used against the lines available, for the label and for the page's fill. An overfull column shows its lines past the last rule: the column's box does not clip, the page's edge does, with `overflow: clip` on the column and the page rather than `hidden`, so the browser never scrolls a column internally to follow a caret past the edge (which would shift the text up and hide the top). The label under the column reads "N lines past the end" in the warning colour while any line is past the last rule, and goes when the text fits; "Page full" is no longer a state. Thumbnails and exports render the same DOM, so they show what the page shows, and lose what the page loses past its edge, as paper would. Each page is one editor with its own document, so nothing flows between pages; that isolation is what makes the continuous editing safe. The font comes from the woff2 subsets Excalidraw ships; a script writes the matching `@font-face` rules at install time. Excalifont has one weight, so bold and italic are the browser's synthetic ones.

**Paragraph alignment.** The alignment attribute gains a fourth value, `"paragraph"`, rendered as `data-align="paragraph"` on the `<p>` (not a `text-align` value) and parsed back; the document validator accepts it, so the backup format goes to version 11 and Dexie to 12 with nothing to convert. The geometry built for the hanging indent stays: the first column's box starts at the page's left inset and carries `hang` = the margin offset less the inset, the second column `hang` 0; the page sets `--column-hang` on each column and `--paragraph-indent` (the first column's hang) on the page. The paragraph rules, shared by the column and the mirror: every paragraph `padding-left: var(--column-hang, 0px)`, so left, centre and right sit right of the rule as they always did; a `data-align="paragraph"` paragraph sets `padding-left: 0` and `text-indent: var(--paragraph-indent)`, so its first line begins at the rule and its wrapped and hard-broken lines run from the left inset. The mirror shares the rules, so capacity counts what the page shows; thumbnails and exports render the same DOM; the divider's limits are unchanged. The format bar gets a fourth alignment button, "Paragraph", with an icon of a first-line indent; no keyboard chord for now (the free Cmd+Shift letters collide with the browsers' own), which the decisions log records. Tab: a keymap binding in the editor inserts four spaces in place of the selection and stops the key, so focus never leaves the page; `white-space: pre-wrap` keeps them; Shift+Tab is claimed by the shell's shortcuts before the editor sees it, as today.

**Preview as a scroll.** The scroll is a column of rows, one per spread, each row the spread's fitted size (the desk's width less its padding, the paper's aspect ratio) with a gap between rows, so the scrollbar is the notebook's true length. Only the rows near the viewport are mounted: the visible ones plus one above and one below (measured with an IntersectionObserver or the scroll position), rendered with the same TextPage and ZinePage as the desk with `readOnly` set and the page's layout kept (rules, margin line, divider) and no `onQuickLine`, `onOpenElement` or `onChange`; every other row is an empty box of the same size in the desk's colour, so a 192-page notebook is a handful of live pages at any moment and scrolling stays smooth. Drawings come from the stills cache as on the desk; nothing is rendered ahead. The row under the middle of the viewport sets the open page through `goTo` (throttled, a page's index from the spread), so the page bar's counter follows and the notebook remembers the place; opening preview scrolls to the open page's spread first. Covers are the cover swatch and the plain inside surfaces the desk shows. The pool panel, drawing mode and the notebook box are closed on entering; the page bar shows only the counter and the overview button in preview; Escape leaves. Exports keep using the `preview` prop on pages off screen as before; the scroll is App state, like drawing mode, kept for the tab.

**Preview keeps the page.** Since 2026-09-19 preview shows the page as it is: the rules, the margin line and the divider are the page's layout, not editing chrome, and they stay. The page prop that hid them, `preview`, is renamed `bare` and is passed by the export path only (`src/notebook/export.tsx`), where it keeps today's export look until the print options make it a choice; the desk's preview passes `readOnly` and never `bare`; the `is-preview` class and its rules become `is-bare`. The renderer strips editing chrome regardless. The overview's blank pages draw their rules and margin line with CSS at the thumbnail's scale from the theme's metrics, so a blank page looks like a blank page.

**Graphite ink.** `Theme.colours` gains `boldInk`; `pageLookStyle` sets `--page-bold-ink` beside `--page-ink`. In `textpage.css` a `strong` without a colour mark takes `color: var(--page-bold-ink)` (the colour mark's inline style wins when there is one, as it already does for the ink), and keeps `font-weight: bold` so faces with a real bold add weight; the mirror shares the rule and nothing about capacity or the format changes. Starting values, to be judged on screen: Ruled and Plain ink `#4a4844` and bold `#1f1e1b` (the old ink), Dark ink `#c9c6bf` and bold `#f4f2ec` (the old ink). The caret follows the ink. The still's drawing ink is Excalidraw's own (`#1e1e1e`) and is not touched, so drawn strokes read as ink, written text as pencil; the quick line's default stroke stays Excalidraw's too.

**Two columns.** Two text areas side by side. The divider is a shell element positioned at the page's divider offset, dragged in 10mm steps. Text does not flow between columns; each column is its own text.

**Text formatting.** Bold, italic, colour and paragraph alignment need a structured document rather than a string, and reliable caret, undo, paste and input-method handling around marks is exactly where hand-rolled editors bleed time. The column is a ProseMirror editor with a tiny schema: a document of paragraphs, each with an alignment attribute; text with bold, italic and colour marks; a hard break node for Shift+Enter; nothing else. Enter splits the paragraph (the new one keeps the alignment and the marks being typed with), so alignment is a block property exactly as in Google Docs: it applies to the whole paragraph (wrapped lines and hard breaks included) and to every paragraph a selection touches, never to a single visual line. The colour palette is Excalidraw's default stroke picks; "black" is the page's ink, stored as no colour mark. Pasted HTML keeps bold, italic, alignment and palette colours and drops every other colour; pasted plain text becomes one paragraph per line, blank lines included. A small bar floats over a selection (after the pointer is released, so it does not chase a drag) with bold, italic, the swatches and the three alignments; the page positions it from the column's report of where the selection is. Capacity is still measured by height; floats for images still work because the editor's content is not a new block formatting context; export still renders the same DOM. Storage: each column keeps a `doc` in ProseMirror's JSON and a plain `text` mirror derived from it (paragraphs and hard breaks as line breaks); the backup format is version 2, with a converter that turns version 1 strings into one paragraph per line (each existing line break becomes a paragraph boundary, since that is what Enter meant before), and the IndexedDB schema upgrades the same way.

**Highlight.** A fourth mark in the schema, `highlight` with a tint id, rendered as a `<mark>` whose background is the theme's colour for that tint (a `--page-highlight-<tint>` custom property set by `pageLookStyle`, so every theme picks its own and Dark can use tints that read on dark paper). The stroke is translucent over ink and rules (`mix-blend-mode: multiply` on light paper, a translucent colour on dark) and covers the glyphs' content box rather than the whole line box, so it reads as a marker line, not a filled cell; `box-decoration-break: clone` keeps wrapped runs whole. It adds no width, so capacity is unchanged. Pasted background colours are dropped (highlights are ours, like colours outside the palette). Backup version 7 and Dexie version 8: the document validator accepts the mark; nothing is converted.

**Clippings (superseded).** Specified 2026-09-18 as free SVG or PNG objects placed on a page from the canvas; superseded the same day by drawing on the page, which gives the page the drawing itself rather than a copy of it. Not built. A clipping is an `<img>` inside the page element, absolutely positioned in mm like everything on the page, so the foreignObject renderer carries it into thumbnails and exports with nothing extra. Layers: the rules are the page's pseudo-elements at z-index 0, the text columns at 1; a clipping "under" paints after the rules at 0, so it sits between the rules and the ink, and "over" paints at 2. Its image is a file in the notebook's files table: SVG from the canvas (Excalidraw's `exportToSvg` over the selected elements with no background; the SVG embeds the fonts its text uses, so a clipping with text is a few hundred KB, deduplicated by content hash), or the clipboard's SVG text or PNG (PNG through the usual import, which keeps transparency). Placement: a new clipping lands centred on the open page at its natural size in scene px, capped at half the page width, on top. Interaction is the page's, outside the editor: pointer down on a clipping selects it (a frame with four corner handles) and drags it; the handles resize keeping the proportions, 5mm wide at least; Backspace or Delete removes a selected one; Escape or a click elsewhere deselects. A clipping over the text takes the pointer where it lies, so text under it is reached by moving it or through the keyboard; a clipping under the text is covered by the column and is reached with Alt (Option) held while clicking, which the page intercepts before the editor. Each selected clipping shows small tools: "Send behind" or "Bring in front", "Delete". The dragging state is local; the page reports the new geometry on release and the session saves it with the page. On dark paper a clipping gets the inversion filter Excalidraw's own dark mode uses, so black ink becomes light ink. The media pool lists clippings' files like any image, with the usage badge; pruning and the delete refusal cover them. Backup version 8 and Dexie version 9: pages gain `clippings` (older pages get an empty array); the `svg` extension in the zip already exists.

**Quick line.** No editor is mounted for it, since mounting Excalidraw on a pointer press would miss the press. Where it lives: `src/page/useQuickLine.ts`, a page-level hook used by TextPage and ZinePage and never by Column, with the pure pieces beside it in `src/page/quickLine.ts` (`snapTo15`, the 4px dead zone, page px to scene px) and their tests, the styles in `src/page/quickline.css`, and the stroke memory in `src/page/drawingMode.ts` next to the aids. The pages take one new prop, `onQuickLine`, and App passes it only to the open page and only while a quick line is allowed (writing mode, no preview, overview and box closed); an absent prop disables the hook, so the other page of a spread and exports' off-screen pages never arm.

Arming: the hook listens on the document in the capture phase for `keydown` with `key === "Shift"` and `repeat` false, and arms only if no pointer button is down (tracked from document pointerdown/up, so a text selection in progress is never interrupted) and the focus is not in a native input, textarea or select and no dialog is open (the same `focusContextOf` the shortcuts use). Disarm: `keyup` of Shift, window `blur`, the document becoming hidden, `keydown` of Tab (so Shift+Tab goes on to toggle drawing mode) or Escape, a focus change into a native field or a dialog, the prop going away (a page change, the overview or the box opening). Armed, the page element gets `is-armed`: `cursor: crosshair` on the page, and `pointer-events: none` on the text columns, the zine cells and blocks, the divider handle and the zine add spots, so the next pointer down reaches the page itself. Shift is a modifier, not a chord: `shortcutFor` matches no binding on `ShiftLeft`/`ShiftRight` and nothing there changes; the hook's own Tab and Escape listeners run before the shortcuts (capture phase) and only disarm, never stop the event, so Shift+Tab still toggles drawing mode.

The drag: pointer down with button 0 and `isPrimary` on the armed page; the page prevents the default of that pointer down (which suppresses the mouse down that would move focus, so the editor keeps its caret) and captures the pointer; the start is the pointer's position in page px, measured from the page's padding box (the border subtracted, as `PageDrawing`'s box is). Moves under 4px from the start show nothing; past that a preview appears: an `<svg class="quick-line page-chrome">` covering the page (inset 0) at z-index 3, above the still (2) and the columns (auto), below the format bar, taking no pointer events, with one `<line>` in the remembered stroke colour and the width times the zoom. The end follows the pointer; `snapTo15` stays in `quickLine.ts`, tested and unused, should snapping return as an option. Pointer up ends the drag: under 4px it is nothing; otherwise the line is reported. Shift released mid-drag disarms but the drag in progress finishes on its pointer up (what was started is finished); Escape mid-drag cancels and disarms; `pointercancel` cancels; a second pointer during a drag is ignored; touch has no Shift and is out. A pen with a keyboard works like a mouse.

The element: `convertToExcalidrawElements([{ type: "line", x, y, points: [[0, 0], [dx, dy]], strokeColor, strokeWidth, roughness }])` with x, y, dx, dy in scene px (page px over the desk's `fit.zoom`), appended to the open page's `drawing` through `session.setDrawing(page.id, { elements: [...drawing, line], files: {} }, session.loadId)`, the same call drawing mode makes, so the per-page saver, the loadId guard, the fill (a drawing alone counts for at least a quarter) and the state update are the ones already there. The still refreshes by itself: `elementsKey` changes with the new element, so `useDrawingStill` builds the new still; it must keep returning the previous still while the new one builds (today it returns null, which would blink the drawing away for a moment), a small change in `stills.ts`. The thumbnail hook's key includes `elementsKey(drawing)`, so the thumbnail re-renders after its quiet moment with nothing added. The stroke: `currentItemStrokeColor`, `currentItemStrokeWidth` and `currentItemRoughness` from the drawing editor's appState, written by `PageDrawing` on each throttled report and on unmount to local storage under `typestill.drawing.stroke`, read by the hook when a line is made; defaults when nothing is stored: Excalidraw's own (`#1e1e1e`, 2, 1). It is a per-browser memory like the aids, not a page or notebook setting, and never in a backup. Undo (2026-09-19): the drawing editor's history begins when it mounts, so quick lines keep their own. The hook keeps, per page and per tab, an action log in memory: a `line` entry with the element's id when a quick line lands, and a `text` entry whenever a column's transaction changes the document and is not itself an undo or redo (Column tells the page through a new `onEdit` callback from `dispatchTransaction`, skipping transactions carrying prosemirror-history's `history$` meta). Cmd/Ctrl+Z, seen by the hook on the document in the capture phase (no Shift, no Alt): if the log's top is a `line`, the event is stopped, the element is removed through `session.setDrawing` (the drawing without that id) and the entry popped; if the top is `text`, the entry is popped and the event goes on to the editor, whose own history undoes the text; if the log is empty, the editor as today. Since the editor groups typing into undo steps of its own, `text` entries can outnumber its steps: when the top is `text` and the focused editor's undo depth (`undoDepth` from prosemirror-history, exposed by Column) is zero, the entry is discarded and the next one looked at, so the walk never stalls. Cmd+Shift+Z is the editor's redo and touches the log only by adding `text` entries as any change does; a removed line has no redo. The log is cleared when the page changes, when drawing mode is entered (the editor owns the elements then), on a reload, and when a page is cleared. Right click while armed: a `pointerdown` with button 2 (and `contextmenu`, prevented while armed) cancels a drag in progress with nothing placed, and otherwise undoes the top `line` entry if there is one, so a line just placed goes with a click of the other button; text entries are not undone by right click.

**Quick shapes.** The hook keeps an `element` for the current Shift hold, `"line"` on arming. While armed it listens on the document in the capture phase for keydown of Alt (`key === "Alt"`, no repeat) with Shift down and no Cmd or Ctrl: it steps the element through line, rectangle, ellipse, arrow and back, and prevents the default of Alt's keydown and keyup while armed, because on Windows and Linux a press-and-release of Alt alone focuses the browser's menu. The shortcuts see nothing (`shortcutFor` needs a code other than a modifier). The indicator is chrome inside the armed page (`page-chrome`, stripped from renders): a small pill at the page's top-right with the element's icon (the shell's icon set gains line, rectangle, ellipse and arrow) and its name, visible while `is-armed`, updated on each cycle. During a drag the preview follows the element: an SVG `<rect>` or `<ellipse>` on the bounding box of start and end (min corner, absolute size; a box under the dead zone in both directions is nothing), a `<line>` for the line, a `<line>` with an arrowhead `<marker>` for the arrow, in the remembered stroke, no fill. On release the element is built by the same converter: `{ type: "rectangle" | "ellipse", x, y, width, height, strokeColor, strokeWidth, roughness, backgroundColor: "transparent" }` in scene px, or `{ type: "arrow", x, y, points: [[0, 0], [dx, dy]], endArrowhead: "arrow", ... }`, appended through `session.setDrawing` as the line is, with the same entry in the action log (the entry names the element, not its kind), so Cmd+Z, right click and cancel are the line's. Releasing Shift resets the element to the line.

**Double-click into drawing mode.** A pure hit test in `src/page/hitTest.ts` (tested): given the page's elements and a point in scene px, the topmost element whose outline is within a tolerance (6 scene px, wider for a thick stroke) of the point: for line, arrow and freedraw the distance to the polyline's segments (points offset by the element's x, y); for rectangle and diamond the distance to the edges; for ellipse the distance to the outline, approximated by the radial distance; for text and image elements the whole box; deleted elements skipped; the last hit in element order wins, as it is drawn on top. The pages listen for `dblclick` in the capture phase on the page element while they are the open page in writing mode with a drawing: the point is taken from the page's padding box over the zoom, the hit test runs, and on a hit the event's default is prevented (no word selection) and the page reports the element's id through a new `onOpenElement` prop; App enters drawing mode and hands `PageDrawing` an `initialSelection` (the id), which it applies through the API once the editor is ready (`updateScene({ appState: { selectedElementIds: { [id]: true } } })`), the same wait the old canvas used for `scrollTo`. No hit: nothing, the double-click is the editor's. Leaving: `PageDrawing` sees Escape (keydown, capture) with the selection tool active and nothing selected, and reports it; App turns drawing mode off. Excalidraw handles its own Escape first (it deselects, or cancels the active tool), so from a selection it takes two presses, which reads as "back, then out".

**Zine pages.** A zine page is a stack of rows, each row one block or two side by side sharing the width equally, laid out from the page size, the padding and the text blocks' rows: a row of text is its rows times the line pitch plus the text inset above and below, and the row with the media block takes what is left, so a taller text block shortens the image; a text block beside the media takes the row's height and as many rows as fit it. Nothing is dragged; composition happens in place through hover affordances: a plus on the empty page, "add below" under the last row, "add beside" on the media block's edges, and each block's own tools (rows, preset, fit, replace, remove). The app allows one media block, one text beside and one text below, though the stored shape is general. The media block renders images with object-fit; text blocks reuse the ProseMirror column with capacity equal to their rows and no rules. A single image alone covers the whole page, including where the page number sits; the number, if on, is drawn over it. Export renders the same DOM; search reads the text blocks; the pool's usage index reads the image and grid blocks; "only an empty page can change kind" means a zine page with no rows. Files are the notebook's existing files table, content-hashed and shared with the canvas; pruning walks zine pages as well as the canvas.

**Images.** Photos would swamp browser storage and backups, so images are downscaled on import with a canvas in the browser (long edge 2048px, JPEG at quality 0.85, PNG kept only when transparent) and stored as data URLs. A 2048px JPEG is a few hundred KB, so a notebook with a hundred images is tens of MB in IndexedDB and in its backup, which browsers handle but is worth showing in notebook settings. Nothing here needs a backend: the owner's browser holds the data and the owner holds the backups; only sync or collaboration would, and both stay "later".

**Media pool.** The pool is a view over the notebook's files table with a usage index: the image references of every zine page plus the file ids of the canvas's image elements, kept in the session and refreshed on save. The panel shows the pool on zine pages, opened from the page bar (since Phase 8; before that it had two modes, canvas and pool). Placing an image on a page writes its file id into the media block; dragging one onto the canvas goes through Excalidraw's API (add the file, then insert an image element). Deletion is refused while the usage index lists the image anywhere, and the pool shows those places. The pool is a CSS grid of square thumbnails (object-fit cover) whose column count follows the panel width, three at least, with 2px gaps; the usage badge is a small pill on the thumbnail, and the delete control appears on hover for unused images only.

**Themes.** The constants in the paper module (pitch, first rule, bottom margin, insets, the line height ratio) and the colour tokens become the fields of a theme, with today's values as the "Ruled" theme, and the page components take the theme as a prop and set the CSS custom properties from it. Two things stop being constants. Fonts load through the FontFace API from bundled files or from a file in the notebook's files table, never from the network. The baseline offset that puts text on the rules is measured at runtime per font and size (the browser reports a font's ascent and descent from a canvas text measurement) and cached, replacing the hand-tuned ratio, so any font lands on the rules without per-font numbers in the theme. Rules are drawn by CSS from the theme as lines, dots or nothing; the mirror-based capacity check does not change. The zine text font is a second font slot on the theme. Export renders the same DOM, so themes carry through to PDF and PNG.

**Page export with identical wrapping.** Render the page's DOM into an SVG foreignObject with the font inlined as data URLs and draw it to a canvas at 2x; the page number is part of the DOM. The same DOM wraps identically on screen and in the export. Print CSS is the fallback if foreignObject proves unreliable.

**Drawing on the page.** The Phase 0 spike already ran Excalidraw pinned to a page: what it taught is reused. The editor mounts over the whole desk (its container must stay wider than about 730px or Excalidraw switches to its mobile layout, and the desk is the window now), and the page is a rectangle inside it: Excalidraw's zoom is set to the page's on-screen zoom (CSS px per scene px, the `fit.zoom` the desk already computes) and its scroll to the page's offset, so scene (0, 0) is the page's top-left corner and one scene px is one page px. Zoom and pan are locked: wheel and pinch are swallowed by a capture-phase listener on the wrapper, the keyboard zoom and pan chords likewise, the hand tool is reset from `onChange`, and any scroll or zoom Excalidraw reports is set back. Drawing outside the page is clipped with `clip-path` on Excalidraw's canvases (real pointer events outside the clip land on the container, as the spike found). The toolbar is stripped to the allowed tools with CSS, since UIOptions cannot hide tools and disabling the image tool breaks paste; Excalidraw's properties panel stays (colours, width, opacity, fill). The grid is off; the rules are the grid. The editor's elements are the page's `drawing`, saved through the page autosave, keyed by Excalidraw's element versions like the canvas was, with the same loadId guard against stale editors. In writing mode the drawing is a still: `exportToSvg` over the elements with no background and a transparent page-sized frame element added so the SVG's viewport is the page, drawn as an `<img>` in the page element (over the text at z-index 2 or under it at 0, per the page's `drawingLayer`), cached per page in memory by the elements' version and refreshed after drawing mode ends; being in the DOM it goes into thumbnails and exports for free. The text and rules opacities in drawing mode are CSS custom properties on the page element, per browser. On dark paper the still gets the inversion filter Excalidraw's dark mode uses, and the live editor takes the `theme` prop. Two ProseMirror columns and one Excalidraw editor never fight: in drawing mode the columns are `pointer-events: none` and read-only; in writing mode the editor is unmounted. The spread shows the other page's still only. Storage: `drawing` and `drawingLayer` are new page fields, empty and "over" for older pages, at the next backup and Dexie versions when this is built; the notebook's `canvas` record, the per-page `canvasView` and the files the canvas references stay as they are.

**Thumbnails.** The same foreignObject render at roughly 120px wide, debounced after each change, stored in IndexedDB keyed by page id.

**Page number and page sides.** The page number is an element inside the page (`src/page/PageMarks.tsx`) in the lined font and ink on every page kind, so thumbnails and exports carry it with no compositing step; the zine font applies to text blocks only. A page's side comes from its position (`src/page/sides.ts`): odd page numbers are right-hand pages, even numbers left-hand, and positions never change. The side picks which corners the page rounds on screen. The page renderer strips it, so thumbnails are stored rectangular like exports, and the overview rounds a thumbnail's outer corners when it shows it. PDF and PNG exports keep the rectangular paper. There is no date stamp; the creation date and time are metadata in page settings.

**Facing pages.** With the panel closed the desk shows the spread the open page belongs to: pages 2k and 2k+1 side by side, the left one rounding its left corners and the right one its right corners, touching at the gutter. Page 1 sits alone on the right with the inside of the front cover on its left, and page N (even) with the inside of the back cover on its right. The other page of the spread is read-only until clicked, when it becomes the open page in the same click. Flipping moves through pages one at a time, so the spread changes when the open page crosses into the next pair. (Divider leaves and blank backs existed between Phase 7 and Phase 10; cuts on sheet boundaries made them unnecessary.)

**Sections.** Pages are slots: the session's `pages` is the notebook's `size` pages in `position` order, and everything walks it (previous and next, numbering, the overview, search, the PDF). Sections are cuts: `sectionOf(sections, position)` is the last section whose `start` is at or below the position, and `sectionRange(sections, size, i)` its run; both pure and tested in `src/notebook/sections.ts`, with the rules that a start is a multiple of `SHEET` (4), the first is 0, starts are strictly increasing and below `size`. Moving a cut, adding one or removing one are edits of the `sections` array alone; no page changes. Growing the notebook appends blank pages (positions `size`…); shrinking refuses unless the pages to drop are blank (by the store's own notion) and no start is at or past the new size. A new notebook is made with all its pages in one transaction. Backup version 10 and Dexie version 11: `size` and `start` arrive, `sectionId` leaves, pages gain `position` and `fill`. Conversion: pages in their Phase 7 order (section order, then `createdAt`); each section's run is padded at its end with blank lined pages from the notebook defaults to the next multiple of 4; positions are assigned in that order; `start` is each section's first position; `size` is the smallest of 64, 96, 128, 192 that holds the total, or the total itself (a multiple of 4) when larger; the remaining slots are blank pages in the last section; `fill` is 0 for blank pages and 0.5 for the rest until the page is next opened and measured. Every page keeps its content, its section and its order; nothing is lost.

**Fill.** Each page keeps `fill`, 0 to 1, so the grid can shade without rendering anything. Lined pages: the lines the text takes over the lines available, summed over the columns (the capacity plugin already measures the document's height in the mirror; the column reports the count on mount and on every change, and the page takes the sum), or at least 0.25 when the page has a drawing and no text. Zine pages: the cells filled and the text blocks written over the cells and blocks present, or 0 with no rows. Written to the page on save, in the same record, so it travels in backups and needs no recomputation. Shades: 0 grey; up to a third light; up to two thirds medium; above that the full tint.

**One page at a time (retired).** From Phase 4 to Phase 9 a predicate, `canAddPage`, refused a new page while the open page was empty. With a notebook of fixed size there is nothing to add, so the rule, its module and the new-page and delete-page paths are removed rather than kept dormant: they have no meaning in the new model.

**Search.** Pages are plain strings. For drawings, walk the page's text elements (they carry `originalText`).

**PDF.** Render each page to PNG at 2x (off screen, at zoom 1, in preview mode, through the same renderer as thumbnails) and place it on a PDF page of the matching physical size (A5 148x210mm, A4 210x297mm, Letter 8.5x11in), respecting orientation, with pdf-lib.

**Multiple notebooks.** Notebook metadata is one record; pages, canvas and files are their own records under the notebook id. The shelf reads only metadata. A notebook is loaded fully when opened.

## 7. Decisions log

- No fork of Excalidraw. Embed the npm package and control it from outside.
- Pages never reorder. Date order is the only order.
- One tag per page.
- Page size and orientation are notebook-level.
- Date stamp and page number are overlays, not canvas elements.
- Browser storage first, backups as downloadable files. No accounts, no backend.
- 2026-09-17: Pages are text only, written in our own editor on lined paper. Drawing lives on one infinite Excalidraw canvas per notebook. This replaces "every page is an Excalidraw canvas" and the paper snapping spec that followed it. Reason: Excalidraw has no document flow and no public way to start editing an element, so a note editor built on it would fight it forever, while its grid mode already gives live snapping for drawings.
- Blank, dotted and grid paper are gone. Pages are lined; the canvas has a grid toggle; "clear" is a view mode.
- One canvas per notebook. Pages remember a canvas view instead of owning canvases.
- A full page stops accepting input. The user creates the next page. (Replaced 2026-09-19 by the continuous page, below.)
- Plain text only on pages in Phase 1. Basic formatting (bold, italic, colour, alignment) is in scope for Phase 2 and moves the page editor onto ProseMirror; columns stay plain strings until then.

Alignment shortcuts follow Google Docs (Cmd+Shift+L / E / R). Bold and italic are synthetic, since Excalifont has one weight; a bolder handwriting face is not planned. Colours outside the palette are dropped on paste. - Dark, built (2026-09-18): the appearance setting is per browser (localStorage, applied before the first paint from index.html), not part of the notebook or its backup, and lives in the App group of the settings dialog. - Clippings (2026-09-18): the canvas is where drawings are made; a clipping is how a drawing comes onto a page, as a free object that overlaps the text without affecting it.
