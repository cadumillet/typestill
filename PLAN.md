# typestill

A digital notebook made to be the bridge between digital notes and real commonplace notebooks and zines. Opinionated but flexible, with simplicity at its core. Built on top of Excalidraw for drawing, on the page itself.

The idea: a notebook of fixed-size lined pages you write on like a simple note editor, in Excalidraw's handwriting font, always on the lines, and draw on with Excalidraw's tools, over and around the writing, as on paper. Writing and drawing are two modes of the same page: in drawing mode the text goes quiet (dimmed or hidden, not clickable) and the page is the canvas, at the page's size, no zoom, no pan. Pages come in two kinds: lined pages for writing, and zine pages for images with a little text. No productivity-app machinery. (Until Phase 8 the drawing surface was one infinite canvas per notebook in a side panel; it stays in the code, unused, see section 8.)

Status (2026-09-18): the MVP is complete. Phases 1, 2 and 3 are done and on `main`. Phase 1: the store, the page editor, the canvas panel and split view, storage wiring with autosave and per-page canvas views, backup download and restore, notebook settings, the left rail and the notebook switcher. Phase 2: text formatting, the notebook cover, zine pages, the media pool, themes (Ruled and Plain), Dark with the app appearance setting, the divider drag, tags, hover thumbnails, the two-page spread, the date stamp and page number, and the page settings popover with the margin line. Phase 3: full-text search, PDF and PNG exports, the zip backup with storage figures and pruning, page deletion, keyboard shortcuts, the first-run welcome and the shelf. The backup format is at version 9 in both its JSON and zip shapes; the IndexedDB schema is at version 10. A feedback round on 2026-09-18 became Phase 4 (section 6): the date stamp descoped from the page, pages with sides and real facing spreads, page controls at the rail with a one-page rule, the media pool as a photo grid, and the zine page rebuilt as blocks composed in place. All five items are on `main`: sides and spreads with the date stamp gone, the page controls at the rail with the one-page rule, the media pool as a photo grid, zine pages as rows of blocks with the format bump, and opening on the last notebook. A second look the same day became Phase 5: one notebook in the UI (the shelf and the switcher go dormant; another notebook is reached by downloading this one and opening its backup), and the page controls moved under the page with the app bar aligned to the page's right edge. Both items are on `main`: the page bar under the page or spread with the rail reduced to squares and the filter and the app bar's right-hand group on the page's right edge; and one notebook in the UI, with the switcher gone, the name in notebook settings and "Open backup…" replacing the open notebook after asking. Section 8 lists what stays in the code without being shown. Phase 6 (specified 2026-09-18) adds a highlight mark to the page text, since Excalifont's synthetic bold barely reads, and clippings: drawings taken from the canvas (or images from the clipboard) placed over or under a lined page's text as free objects, moved and resized by hand. The highlight is on `main` (backup version 7, Dexie version 8); clippings were superseded, see Phase 8. Phase 7 (specified 2026-09-18, built the same day after Phase 8) turns tags into sections, the physical divisions of a notebook: every page belongs to a section, sections order the pages, the rail is one column per section, and the spread shows section dividers as leaves; its zine feature flag (`src/features.ts`, off in production builds) came first. Backup version 9 and Dexie version 10 convert tags in place. Phase 8 (specified 2026-09-18, built the same day, before Phase 7's sections) is the pivot to drawing on the page: the side canvas leaves the UI and every page carries its own drawing, made in a drawing mode where the writing is locked and can be dimmed; this supersedes the clippings item of Phase 6. Its first item is built: backup version 8 and Dexie version 9 carry the page drawings, drawing mode is on the page bar (⌥D), the still is in thumbnails and exports, and the infinite canvas is dormant (section 8); the cleanup item stays open. Phase 9 (specified 2026-09-18 from the owner's look at the branch, not built) takes the page number off the page, regroups the page bar, folds the rail, the settings dialog and the page settings into one notebook box with the map, trims the drawing tools, gives divider backs the section's tab, and opens section 9, the list of features that may be descoped before release. ## 1. Product principles

- It should feel like a Moleskine with a pen and a pencil, not like a productivity app.
- Writing and drawing are different activities and get different modes of the same page: the editor for text, Excalidraw for everything drawn, one on top of the other.
- Pages are appended in creation order within their section. Never reordered by hand.
- Pages have no title. They have a date, a section, and content.
- The app is not opinionated about organization. Sections, the notebook's physical divisions, are the only structure.
- Text is on the lines by construction. The user never nudges things to keep a page tidy.
- Everything drawn is plain Excalidraw data. We only build the shell around it.
- Simplicity over features. Each page kind does one thing well: lined pages hold writing, zine pages hold images. Images never go into lined pages.
- The owner holds the data: browser storage plus backup files they keep. No accounts, no backend.
- It behaves like a real notebook in human hands. Things stay tied together, and the user plays by its rules rather than the app quietly untying them.
- The spread is the notebook's true unit. Pages have sides, pairs face each other, covers have insides, and printing a notebook means printing spreads. Decisions about pages keep spreads first-class.

## 2. Product spec

### Notebooks
- One notebook at a time. Opening typestill is opening your current commonplace notebook, where it was left; there is no list to choose from. To work on a different notebook, download this one as a backup and open the other one's backup file: the opened notebook takes the current one's place (after asking, since the current one is only kept by its backup). A first run still makes the first notebook through the welcome dialog, or opens a backup.
- The storage stays multi-notebook underneath (a notebook is a record with its own pages, canvas and files, and the shelf and switcher remain in the code, see section 8); the UI simply shows one. The name is set in notebook settings.
- A notebook has many pages and exactly one canvas.
- Every notebook has a cover: a colour from a small palette, an optional emoji or initial, the name, and an optional subtitle. The cover is what the notebook looks like when closed: a small swatch next to the name in the app bar, and the card on the shelf when the shelf is shown again one day. It is set in notebook settings. Image covers are not planned.

### Layout
- The text column on the left is the app. Its app bar holds the wordmark at the left and, at the right, one group whose right edge lines up with the right edge of the page or spread on the desk, whatever the column's width: the notebook's cover swatch and name (a button that opens notebook settings), search, the notebook menu (settings, exports, backup), preview and the canvas panel toggle. Controls are icon buttons with a hover tooltip showing the label and, once one exists, the keyboard shortcut.
- Under the page or spread sits the page bar, as wide as the page or spread. In its middle, centred on the page: previous, the page counter, next. The counter is a button showing the open page's section as a coloured dot with the number ("● 3 / 12"); it opens the notebook box on the map. At its left end: new page (lined, or lined or zine behind the flag) and delete page. At its right end: the layout toggle (two columns, on lined pages) and drawing mode. The bar belongs to the page the way a caption does, so it moves with the page when the desk is resized, and the page is fitted above it.
- The desk is the whole window below the app bar, with the page or spread centred; there is no rail (the notebook's map lives in the notebook box, see below). A rounded side panel on the right, resized from the gap between the two with its width remembered per browser, exists only for the media pool on zine pages (it opens from the page bar there and closes with the page). The canvas panel is gone; drawing happens on the page.
- One box holds everything about the notebook: the notebook box, a dialog opened from the app bar's cover-and-name button, from the page bar's counter, and with ⌥M. Top to bottom: the map (the notebook as one column per section, side by side in section order, a tab in the section's colour with its name over a stack of small squares, one per page; hovering a square previews the page; clicking one opens it and closes the box; the open page's square is the full colour, the others a tint), the Sections group (name, colour, order, add, delete), the This page group (the open page's section, its layout, its kind behind the flag, when it was created), and the notebook's own groups (name and cover, paper size and orientation, theme, the new-page defaults, the app appearance, storage). Changes apply as they are made; there is no Save. It replaces the notebook settings dialog, the page settings popover and the rail, so notebook and page settings stop being two places.
- The text column shows one page, fit to the available space at the paper's aspect ratio. With the panel closed it shows the open page's spread: a left-hand page with the next page on its right, the two touching at the gutter with no gap, as a notebook lies open. The notebook is a sequence of sides: the inside of the front cover, then for each section its divider leaf (front and back) and its pages, then the inside of the back cover; sides alternate left and right from the inside cover, so a divider's front and every section's first page are right-hand pages, and a section ending on a right-hand page is followed by the blank back of that leaf. Page 1 is thus a right-hand page and the spreads run (inside cover | divider), (divider back | 1), (2 | 3) and so on. An empty side of a spread, the inside of a cover, shows a flat page-sized surface in the chrome's colours (white in the light appearance, dark grey in the dark one), with nothing on it and nothing to click; a divider leaf carries its section on both faces, as a real divider's tab shows from either side: the front is a slab in the section's colour with the name, the back is paper with a tab in the section's colour along its outer edge bearing the name, so the first page of a section opens with its section on the left and the page on the right without the wall a full-colour back made; a blank back is paper with nothing on it. None of these is numbered or clickable. The page of a spread that is not open is dimmed until it is clicked. This is the ground for a second cover, an inside cover and a counter cover later.
- A page has two modes, writing and drawing, switched from the page bar (a pencil, Shift+Tab) and remembered per browser for the session; see Drawing.

### Pages (text)
- Page size is set per notebook: A5, A4 or Letter. Orientation is set per notebook: portrait or landscape.
- A page is lined paper: horizontal rules at a fixed pitch, a top margin, and a light red margin line on the left. Text sits on the rules. The margin line position is a notebook setting (20mm to begin with) that every page follows, as one bound notebook has one margin; it is no longer adjusted per page (the per-page field stays stored, unused, see section 8).
- Writing works like a simple note editor: typing, line breaks, wrapping at the column edge, caret, selection, undo, copy and paste. Plain text only. The font is Excalifont, Excalidraw's handwriting font.
- A page holds a fixed number of lines. When it is full, input stops. The user creates the next page.
- Optional divider: a light gray vertical line that splits the page into two columns of text. It defaults to the middle of the writable area (from the margin line to the right edge), snaps to 10mm steps when moved, and can be removed. A new page inherits the divider of the page it was created from. "Two columns" is toggled from the page settings popover, next to the page's metadata (number, date).
- The page is a white sheet with a thin border, no shadow. Every page has a side, derived from its position and never stored: odd pages are right-hand pages, even pages left-hand. A right-hand page rounds its right corners and a left-hand page its left corners; the inner edge is square, in single-page view too.
- Basic formatting: bold, italic, text colour from a small palette shared with the canvas (Excalidraw's five stroke colours), highlight, and paragraph alignment (left, centre, right). Alignment works like Google Docs: it applies to the whole paragraph, including its wrapped lines and any line breaks inside it, and to every paragraph a selection touches. Enter starts a new paragraph; Shift+Enter breaks a line within the paragraph. A small bar floats over a selection with bold, italic, the five swatches, the highlighter and the three alignments; the shortcuts are Cmd+B, Cmd+I, Cmd+Shift+H and Cmd+Shift+L / E / R, as in Google Docs. Nothing else: no font sizes, lists or links. The text stays on the lines whatever the formatting.
- Highlight: a marker stroke behind a run of text, translucent, so the ink and the rule show through as under a real highlighter. Four tints shared with the canvas (Excalidraw's element background picks: yellow, green, blue, pink), yellow first; each theme maps the tints to its paper, so Dark has its own. The format bar's highlighter button applies the last tint used (yellow to begin with) and toggles it off on a highlighted selection; its swatches pick a tint or none. Cmd+Shift+H does the same as the button. Bold stays as it is: synthetic and faint in Excalifont, real in Plain's typeface and in zine text.
- Pages are added and deleted from the page bar under the page: new page (lined or zine) and delete page, which asks first. Remaining pages are renumbered, since the number is just the position in the notebook. A new page cannot be added while the open page is empty; the user fills the page in front of them first.
- Nothing is printed on the page but what the user puts there: no page number and no date stamp. The page bar says which page is open, and the creation date and time show in the notebook box. Numbering the pages of an export is a later option of the export itself (the stored `showPageNumber` fields wait for it, unused, see section 8). (The page number was inside the page from Phase 2 to Phase 8; the date stamp existed in Phases 2 and 3.)
- Preview: the page without rules, margin and divider, read-only.

### Pages (zine)
- A second page kind, for images, next to lined pages. A notebook mixes both freely. The kind is chosen when a page is created and can change only while the page is empty.
- A zine page is composed in place from blocks, like a Behance project but small. An empty page shows a plus on hover; clicking it offers a single image, a grid or a text block. Once a block is there, hovering below it offers what can go beneath, and hovering the left or right edge of the media block offers a text block beside it. Nothing is dragged into position.
- At most one media block per page (a single image or a grid of two to four), at most one text block beside it and one below it. A single image with nothing else fills the whole page, page number area included. A text block below reserves rows beneath the media; the taller the text block, the shorter the image, which stays cropped to cover. Two blocks in a row split the width in half.
- Padding is the only layout setting left in page settings: zero (the default) bleeds images to the page edges; otherwise the same inset on every side and the same gap between blocks. Everything about a block (its rows of text, a grid's preset, an image's fit or replacement, removing it) is in the tools that appear when the block is hovered.
- Text blocks use the same editor as lined pages with the same formatting and the same hard stop when full, but no rules and the theme's zine font, a typeface rather than the handwriting of lined pages. A text block's height is its rows of text, default four, changed from its hover tools.
- Grid presets: two side by side, two stacked, two by two. Each cell fills with its image cropped to cover it; a per-image "fit" option letterboxes instead. Empty cells show a placeholder until an image is dropped in.
- Images come from paste, drop or a file picker. They are downscaled on import (long edge 2048px, re-encoded), stored once per notebook by content hash in the same files table the canvas uses, and inlined in backups.
- Zine pages remember a canvas view like lined pages, appear in the rail like any page, face each other in the two-page spread, and export at physical size like lined pages.
- Zine pages are behind a feature flag, so the app can ship with lined pages and the canvas first and zine pages in a later release. With the flag off, nothing makes a new zine page (the New page control makes a lined page outright, the ⌥⇧N shortcut does nothing, page settings show no Kind row); a zine page already in the notebook (from a backup made with the flag on) still opens, edits and exports as ever, with the pool beside it, so no data is hidden or lost. The flag is a build-time setting (`VITE_FEATURE_ZINE`, on in development, off in production builds) with a per-browser override in local storage for looking at either state in either build. It goes away when zine pages ship.

### Media pool
- Every notebook has one media pool: every image in the notebook, whether placed on a zine page or pasted into a page's drawing. One image is one record, by content hash, however many places use it.
- The side panel shows the pool while a zine page is open; it is the panel's only content now that drawing happens on the page.
- Images enter the pool by dropping files on the pool or on a page, by paste, or with a file picker. They are downscaled on import (see section 5). The same file imported twice is one image.
- An image is placed by dragging it from the pool onto a cell of the page's media block, or by selecting a cell and clicking an image. Dropping onto a filled cell replaces the image there. (Dragging an image onto the infinite canvas went dormant with it, section 8.)
- The pool looks like a phone's photo library: square thumbnails edge to edge in a tight grid of three or more columns, newest first, no captions. Only a slim bar sits above it: the peek control, an add button (drop and paste work anywhere on the pool), and the count.
- Usage is a small badge on the thumbnail, always visible: "p. 2", "3 pages", "canvas", or nothing when unused. An image in use cannot be deleted; the user replaces it first. Unused images show a delete control on hover, which asks first and removes the image from the notebook.

### Themes
- A theme is the look of the pages and nothing else: the fonts, the line grid, the rules and margin line, the colours, the zine defaults. The app's chrome is not themed.
- Every notebook references one theme. Changing it re-lays out every page at once; text keeps its lines, and the usual rule for text past the last line applies.
- Built-in themes come with the app. "Ruled" is today's look: Excalifont on lines at 7mm with the red margin line. "Plain" is a monospaced typeface with no visible rules and no margin line, on the same invisible grid. Rules can be lines, dots or none; the grid and the hard stop are always there, since they are the constraint, not the decoration.
- "Dark" is the third built-in theme, right after the first two: dark paper, light ink, dimmed rules and margin line, the same five colour picks. Because the pages are themed and the chrome is not, Dark comes with an app appearance setting, light, dark or system, that the chrome and the canvas follow (Excalidraw has its own dark theme). Picking the Dark theme suggests the dark appearance; the two stay separate settings.
- Custom themes come later: made in settings from the same fields, with user-provided font files stored in the notebook, travelling in backups. A notebook template, meaning a theme plus notebook defaults and a page size, is also later.

### Drawing (on the page)
- Every page, lined or zine, carries a drawing: plain Excalidraw elements in the page's own coordinates (scene px from the page's top-left corner, the same millimetre scale as everything on the page). Drawing mode puts an Excalidraw editor over the open page at exactly the page's size and the page's on-screen zoom: no zoom, no pan, the page is the canvas. Strokes keep their physical size whatever the window.
- In drawing mode the writing is locked: the text and the rules stay visible but take no clicks, so drawing over them is easy. Two dimming controls sit with the drawing tools, "Text" and "Rules", each full, dim or hidden; they are ways of seeing while drawing, remembered per browser, never stored with the page and never applied to exports. Highlighting is drawing: a wide translucent stroke over a line of text.
- Tools: selection, pen, line, arrow, rectangle, ellipse, text, eraser, with Excalidraw's own stroke, fill, width and opacity choices. Images come in by paste and become image elements in the drawing, stored in the notebook's files table. Hand, frames, embeds, the laser and the rest are hidden. What is drawn may run past the page edge; the page clips it.
- In writing mode the drawing shows as a still picture over the text, and the text is editable again. There is no over-or-under choice: each drawn element has its own opacity in Excalidraw, which is how a drawing sits back (the stored `drawingLayer` is unused, see section 8). Thumbnails, the preview and exports show writing and drawing together, always at full opacity.
- Drawing mode is for the open page only; the other page of a spread shows its drawing still. Entering drawing mode on a zine page closes the pool panel.
- The infinite canvas of Phases 1 to 5 stays in the data and in backups, unused (section 8): nothing drawn there is lost, and nothing is moved onto pages by itself.

### Sections
- A section is a division of the notebook, as the tabbed dividers of a physical notebook make: a journal, work notes and reflections can share one commonplace notebook and still be found at once. Every page belongs to exactly one section; every notebook has at least one. (Sections replace tags, which were one label per page or none.)
- Sections have a name, a colour from the cover palette, and an order, all set in the notebook box, where they are added, renamed, recoloured, reordered and deleted. Deleting a section moves its pages to the section before it (or after, for the first) after a confirm naming the count; the last section cannot be deleted. A page's section is changed in the box's This page group, where "New section…" also lives.
- The order of pages is section order first, then creation order within the section. Page numbers run through the whole notebook in that order, so a section is a contiguous run of pages, as in a bound notebook. A page moved to another section takes its place there by its creation date.
- The section colour colours the page's square in the map, the dot in the page bar's counter, and the section's divider leaf in the spread.
- New page adds to the section of the page in front of you. A section may hold no pages; the map still shows its column, and its tab creates its first page (subject to the one-page rule).
- Each section remembers the page last opened in it, so its tab returns there; a section not yet visited opens at its last page, where writing continues.

### The map (in the notebook box)
- The notebook seen whole, like zooming out: one column per section, side by side in section order, a tab at the top in the section's colour with its name, then a vertical stack of small squares, one per page of the section, in order. Think GitHub contribution squares, one column per divider of the notebook. It sits at the top of the notebook box (see Layout); the rail it grew from is gone from the desk.
- Squares are a light tint of their section's colour; the open page's square is the colour itself.
- Hovering a square shows its label (page number and section) and a minified preview of the page.
- Clicking a square opens the page and closes the box. Clicking a tab opens the section where it was left (see Sections) and closes the box.
- Quick ways in: ⌥M, or the counter in the page bar.

### Search
- Full-text search across all pages, their writing and the text drawn on them. Results jump to the page.

### Storage
- Browser storage (IndexedDB), autosave on every change.
- Download the notebook as a backup file. Open a backup file to restore: the opened notebook becomes the current one, after asking, and the notebook it replaces stays only in whatever backup was downloaded of it.

### Export
- PDF of all pages, without rules, margin or divider, writing and drawing together. Page number included when enabled.
- PNG of the current page, same treatment.

### Platform
- Desktop browsers only.
- Personal use first, but built so it can be shipped to others.
- Keep the door open for sync and collaboration later. Do not build them now.

## 3. Data model

```
Notebook {
  id: string
  name: string
  createdAt: number
  lastOpenedAt: number
  lastPageId: string | null            // the page the notebook opens at
  cover: { color: string, emoji: string | null, subtitle: string | null }
  themeId: string                      // a built-in theme, or later a custom one
  pageSize: "A5" | "A4" | "Letter"
  orientation: "portrait" | "landscape"
  defaults: {
    showPageNumber: boolean
    margin: number                     // margin line offset in mm for new pages
    divider: number | null
  }
  sections: Section[]                  // in order; at least one
}

Section {
  id: string
  name: string
  color: string                        // from the cover palette
  lastPageId: string | null            // where the section was left; its tab returns there
}

Page {
  id: string
  notebookId: string
  createdAt: number                    // also the page order
  kind: "lined" | "zine"
  sectionId: string                    // every page is in a section; pages order by (section order, createdAt)
  showPageNumber: boolean
  margin: number                       // margin line offset in mm
  columns: Column[]                    // lined pages: one or two columns
  zine?: Zine                          // zine pages
  divider: number | null               // mm from the left edge, null for one column
  drawing: ExcalidrawElement[]         // the page's drawing, in page coordinates; empty for none
  drawingLayer: "over" | "under"       // where the still drawing paints in writing mode
  canvasView: { scrollX, scrollY, zoom } | null   // dormant with the infinite canvas (section 8)
}

Column {
  text: string                         // plain text mirror of doc: search, and what a backup reader without the editor can use
  doc: EditorDocument                  // ProseMirror JSON: paragraphs with an align attr; text with bold, italic, colour and highlight marks; hard breaks
}                                      // the highlight mark's attr is a tint id: "yellow" | "green" | "blue" | "pink"

Zine {
  padding: number                      // mm; 0 = images bleed to the page edges; also the gap between blocks
  rows: ZineRow[]                      // top to bottom; an empty page has none
}

ZineRow { blocks: ZineBlock[] }        // one block, or two side by side sharing the width equally

ZineBlock =
  | { kind: "image", image: { fileId: string, fit: "cover" | "contain" } | null }
  | { kind: "grid", layout: "row" | "column" | "square", images: ({ fileId, fit } | null)[] }   // 2 side by side, 2 stacked, 2 by 2
  | { kind: "text", column: Column, rows: number }                                              // rows of text at the zine line height
                                       // the shape is general; the app allows one media block, one text beside it, one text below

Theme {
  id: string
  name: string
  lined: {
    font: Font
    pitchMm: number                    // rule pitch, and the text line height
    firstRuleMm: number
    bottomMm: number                   // no rule closer than this to the bottom edge
    rules: "lines" | "dots" | "none"
    marginLine: boolean
    defaultMarginMm: number
    textInsetMm: number
    rightInsetMm: number
  }
  zine: { font: Font, defaultPaddingMm: number, defaultTextRows: number }
  colours: { paper: string, ink: string, rule: string, margin: string, divider: string }
  page: { border: boolean, cornerMm: number }
}

Font {
  family: string
  source: "bundled" | "file"           // a font shipped with the app, or a file in the notebook
  fileId?: string
  lineHeight: number                   // unitless; the font size is the pitch divided by it
}                                      // the baseline offset is measured at runtime, never stored

Canvas {                               // the infinite canvas of Phases 1 to 5: kept, dormant (section 8)
  notebookId: string                   // one per notebook
  gridEnabled: boolean
  elements: ExcalidrawElement[]        // untouched Excalidraw data
}

NotebookFile { notebookId, id, data: BinaryFileData }   // images, referenced by zine pages, page drawings and the dormant canvas
```

Notes:
- Pages, the canvas and files are separate records so autosave writes only what changed. The shelf reads notebook metadata only.
- A page's `drawing` (and the dormant canvas's `elements`) is valid Excalidraw data. It can be exported as a normal `.excalidraw` file at any time, and future sync/collab can reuse Excalidraw's own reconciliation instead of a custom one.
- Backup file = notebook, pages, canvas and files as one JSON document, images base64 inside; or, once the notebook has images, a zip with the same JSON as `notebook.json` (file entries without their data) and each image as `files/<id>.<ext>`. Both open the same way. Format version 9 (version 9 replaces `tags` and `tagId` with `sections` and `sectionId`, converted from older files by `sectionsFromTags`; version 8 adds each page's `drawing` and `drawingLayer`, empty and "over" in older files; version 7 only marks that documents may carry the highlight mark; nothing else is converted); version 1 files (plain-string columns) are converted on open, each line break becoming a paragraph boundary, files from before version 3 get the default cover, pages from before version 4 are lined, notebooks from before version 5 use the Ruled theme, and zine pages from before version 6 are converted to rows of blocks with their date stamp fields dropped. The IndexedDB schema has the same upgrades (Dexie versions 2 to 10).
- Page thumbnails are cached separately in IndexedDB (a `thumbnails` table, Dexie version 6) and are not part of the file.
- Version 6 of the backup format and Dexie version 7 (Phase 4) removed the date stamp fields and store zine pages as rows of blocks: the old media block becomes the first row, with the text beside it as that row's second block on the side it had; the text below becomes a second row with the same rows of text; a page with no images and no writing becomes an empty page. Nothing is lost (`src/page/zineLegacy.ts`). The date stamp's removal and the zine change made one migration rather than two.
- Built-in themes are code, not records. Custom themes (later) are records under the notebook and go into the backup with it.

## 4. Tech stack

- React + TypeScript + Vite, pnpm
- `@excalidraw/excalidraw` (MIT) for the canvas, embedded, not forked
- Our own page editor in Excalifont, self-hosted from Excalidraw's font files: ProseMirror with a tiny schema (paragraphs with alignment; marks bold, italic, colour, highlight; a hard break)
- Dexie for IndexedDB
- Excalidraw's `exportToBlob` / `exportToCanvas` for the canvas; SVG foreignObject rendering for pages
- pdf-lib to assemble page images into a PDF

## 5. How the tricky parts get solved

**Text on the lines.** One editor per column, in Excalifont, with the line height equal to the rule pitch and the top placed so the first baseline sits on the first rule; paragraphs have zero margin, so every baseline lands on a rule whatever the formatting. Wrapping at the column edge and everything else (caret, selection, input methods) is the browser's; ProseMirror keeps the document and the DOM in step. A contenteditable rather than a text area so text can later wrap around images floated in the column. Capacity is the number of rules on the page: every transaction that changes the document is measured first, by rendering the candidate document into a hidden mirror laid out like the column (with the trailing break ProseMirror adds to an empty last line), and one that would push text past the last rule is dropped. Text already past the last rule (after a page size change) can still be edited as long as the edit does not make it longer. The font comes from the woff2 subsets Excalidraw ships; a script writes the matching `@font-face` rules at install time. Excalifont has one weight, so bold and italic are the browser's synthetic ones.

**Two columns.** Two text areas side by side. The divider is a shell element positioned at the page's divider offset, dragged in 10mm steps. Text does not flow between columns; each column is its own text.

**Text formatting.** Bold, italic, colour and paragraph alignment need a structured document rather than a string, and reliable caret, undo, paste and input-method handling around marks is exactly where hand-rolled editors bleed time. The column is a ProseMirror editor with a tiny schema: a document of paragraphs, each with an alignment attribute; text with bold, italic and colour marks; a hard break node for Shift+Enter; nothing else. Enter splits the paragraph (the new one keeps the alignment and the marks being typed with), so alignment is a block property exactly as in Google Docs: it applies to the whole paragraph (wrapped lines and hard breaks included) and to every paragraph a selection touches, never to a single visual line. The colour palette is Excalidraw's default stroke picks; "black" is the page's ink, stored as no colour mark. Pasted HTML keeps bold, italic, alignment and palette colours and drops every other colour; pasted plain text becomes one paragraph per line, blank lines included. A small bar floats over a selection (after the pointer is released, so it does not chase a drag) with bold, italic, the swatches and the three alignments; the page positions it from the column's report of where the selection is. Capacity is still measured by height; floats for images still work because the editor's content is not a new block formatting context; export still renders the same DOM. Storage: each column keeps a `doc` in ProseMirror's JSON and a plain `text` mirror derived from it (paragraphs and hard breaks as line breaks); the backup format is version 2, with a converter that turns version 1 strings into one paragraph per line (each existing line break becomes a paragraph boundary, since that is what Enter meant before), and the IndexedDB schema upgrades the same way.

**Highlight.** A fourth mark in the schema, `highlight` with a tint id, rendered as a `<mark>` whose background is the theme's colour for that tint (a `--page-highlight-<tint>` custom property set by `pageLookStyle`, so every theme picks its own and Dark can use tints that read on dark paper). The stroke is translucent over ink and rules (`mix-blend-mode: multiply` on light paper, a translucent colour on dark) and covers the glyphs' content box rather than the whole line box, so it reads as a marker line, not a filled cell; `box-decoration-break: clone` keeps wrapped runs whole. It adds no width, so capacity is unchanged. Pasted background colours are dropped (highlights are ours, like colours outside the palette). Backup version 7 and Dexie version 8: the document validator accepts the mark; nothing is converted.

**Clippings (superseded).** Specified 2026-09-18 as free SVG or PNG objects placed on a page from the canvas; superseded the same day by drawing on the page, which gives the page the drawing itself rather than a copy of it. Not built. A clipping is an `<img>` inside the page element, absolutely positioned in mm like everything on the page, so the foreignObject renderer carries it into thumbnails and exports with nothing extra. Layers: the rules are the page's pseudo-elements at z-index 0, the text columns at 1; a clipping "under" paints after the rules at 0, so it sits between the rules and the ink, and "over" paints at 2. Its image is a file in the notebook's files table: SVG from the canvas (Excalidraw's `exportToSvg` over the selected elements with no background; the SVG embeds the fonts its text uses, so a clipping with text is a few hundred KB, deduplicated by content hash), or the clipboard's SVG text or PNG (PNG through the usual import, which keeps transparency). Placement: a new clipping lands centred on the open page at its natural size in scene px, capped at half the page width, on top. Interaction is the page's, outside the editor: pointer down on a clipping selects it (a frame with four corner handles) and drags it; the handles resize keeping the proportions, 5mm wide at least; Backspace or Delete removes a selected one; Escape or a click elsewhere deselects. A clipping over the text takes the pointer where it lies, so text under it is reached by moving it or through the keyboard; a clipping under the text is covered by the column and is reached with Alt (Option) held while clicking, which the page intercepts before the editor. Each selected clipping shows small tools: "Send behind" or "Bring in front", "Delete". The dragging state is local; the page reports the new geometry on release and the session saves it with the page. On dark paper a clipping gets the inversion filter Excalidraw's own dark mode uses, so black ink becomes light ink. The media pool lists clippings' files like any image, with the usage badge; pruning and the delete refusal cover them. Backup version 8 and Dexie version 9: pages gain `clippings` (older pages get an empty array); the `svg` extension in the zip already exists.

**Zine pages.** A zine page is a stack of rows, each row one block or two side by side sharing the width equally, laid out from the page size, the padding and the text blocks' rows: a row of text is its rows times the line pitch plus the text inset above and below, and the row with the media block takes what is left, so a taller text block shortens the image; a text block beside the media takes the row's height and as many rows as fit it. Nothing is dragged; composition happens in place through hover affordances: a plus on the empty page, "add below" under the last row, "add beside" on the media block's edges, and each block's own tools (rows, preset, fit, replace, remove). The app allows one media block, one text beside and one text below, though the stored shape is general. The media block renders images with object-fit; text blocks reuse the ProseMirror column with capacity equal to their rows and no rules. A single image alone covers the whole page, including where the page number sits; the number, if on, is drawn over it. Export renders the same DOM; search reads the text blocks; the pool's usage index reads the image and grid blocks; "only an empty page can change kind" means a zine page with no rows. Files are the notebook's existing files table, content-hashed and shared with the canvas; pruning walks zine pages as well as the canvas.

**Images.** Photos would swamp browser storage and backups, so images are downscaled on import with a canvas in the browser (long edge 2048px, JPEG at quality 0.85, PNG kept only when transparent) and stored as data URLs. A 2048px JPEG is a few hundred KB, so a notebook with a hundred images is tens of MB in IndexedDB and in its backup, which browsers handle but is worth showing in notebook settings. Nothing here needs a backend: the owner's browser holds the data and the owner holds the backups; only sync or collaboration would, and both stay "later".

**Media pool.** The pool is a view over the notebook's files table with a usage index: the image references of every zine page plus the file ids of the canvas's image elements, kept in the session and refreshed on save. The panel shows the pool on zine pages, opened from the page bar (since Phase 8; before that it had two modes, canvas and pool). Placing an image on a page writes its file id into the media block; dragging one onto the canvas goes through Excalidraw's API (add the file, then insert an image element). Deletion is refused while the usage index lists the image anywhere, and the pool shows those places. The pool is a CSS grid of square thumbnails (object-fit cover) whose column count follows the panel width, three at least, with 2px gaps; the usage badge is a small pill on the thumbnail, and the delete control appears on hover for unused images only.

**Themes.** The constants in the paper module (pitch, first rule, bottom margin, insets, the line height ratio) and the colour tokens become the fields of a theme, with today's values as the "Ruled" theme, and the page components take the theme as a prop and set the CSS custom properties from it. Two things stop being constants. Fonts load through the FontFace API from bundled files or from a file in the notebook's files table, never from the network. The baseline offset that puts text on the rules is measured at runtime per font and size (the browser reports a font's ascent and descent from a canvas text measurement) and cached, replacing the hand-tuned ratio, so any font lands on the rules without per-font numbers in the theme. Rules are drawn by CSS from the theme as lines, dots or nothing; the mirror-based capacity check does not change. The zine text font is a second font slot on the theme. Export renders the same DOM, so themes carry through to PDF and PNG.

**Page export with identical wrapping.** Render the page's DOM into an SVG foreignObject with the font inlined as data URLs and draw it to a canvas at 2x; the page number is part of the DOM. The same DOM wraps identically on screen and in the export. Print CSS is the fallback if foreignObject proves unreliable.

**Drawing on the page.** The Phase 0 spike already ran Excalidraw pinned to a page: what it taught is reused. The editor mounts over the whole desk (its container must stay wider than about 730px or Excalidraw switches to its mobile layout, and the desk is the window now), and the page is a rectangle inside it: Excalidraw's zoom is set to the page's on-screen zoom (CSS px per scene px, the `fit.zoom` the desk already computes) and its scroll to the page's offset, so scene (0, 0) is the page's top-left corner and one scene px is one page px. Zoom and pan are locked: wheel and pinch are swallowed by a capture-phase listener on the wrapper, the keyboard zoom and pan chords likewise, the hand tool is reset from `onChange`, and any scroll or zoom Excalidraw reports is set back. Drawing outside the page is clipped with `clip-path` on Excalidraw's canvases (real pointer events outside the clip land on the container, as the spike found). The toolbar is stripped to the allowed tools with CSS, since UIOptions cannot hide tools and disabling the image tool breaks paste; Excalidraw's properties panel stays (colours, width, opacity, fill). The grid is off; the rules are the grid. The editor's elements are the page's `drawing`, saved through the page autosave, keyed by Excalidraw's element versions like the canvas was, with the same loadId guard against stale editors. In writing mode the drawing is a still: `exportToSvg` over the elements with no background and a transparent page-sized frame element added so the SVG's viewport is the page, drawn as an `<img>` in the page element (over the text at z-index 2 or under it at 0, per the page's `drawingLayer`), cached per page in memory by the elements' version and refreshed after drawing mode ends; being in the DOM it goes into thumbnails and exports for free. The text and rules opacities in drawing mode are CSS custom properties on the page element, per browser. On dark paper the still gets the inversion filter Excalidraw's dark mode uses, and the live editor takes the `theme` prop. Two ProseMirror columns and one Excalidraw editor never fight: in drawing mode the columns are `pointer-events: none` and read-only; in writing mode the editor is unmounted. The spread shows the other page's still only. Storage: `drawing` and `drawingLayer` are new page fields, empty and "over" for older pages, at the next backup and Dexie versions when this is built; the notebook's `canvas` record, the per-page `canvasView` and the files the canvas references stay as they are.

**Thumbnails.** The same foreignObject render at roughly 120px wide, debounced after each change, stored in IndexedDB keyed by page id.

**Page number and page sides.** The page number is an element inside the page (`src/page/PageMarks.tsx`) in the lined font and ink on every page kind, so thumbnails and exports carry it with no compositing step; the zine font applies to text blocks only. A page's side comes from its position in the notebook's side sequence (`src/page/sides.ts`, see Sections below): the inside of the front cover is side 0, a left-hand side, and sides alternate from there, so odd side indexes are right-hand. The side picks which corners the page rounds on screen. The page renderer strips it, so thumbnails are stored rectangular like exports, and the rail rounds a thumbnail's outer corners when it shows it, from the page's position at that moment: deleting a page flips the sides of every page after it, and no stored image goes stale. PDF and PNG exports keep the rectangular paper. There is no date stamp; the creation date and time are metadata in page settings.

**Facing pages.** With the panel closed the desk shows the spread the open page belongs to: the side before or after it in the side sequence, the left one rounding its left corners and the right one its right corners, touching at the gutter. The other side may be a page (read-only until clicked, when it becomes the open page in the same click), a divider leaf, a blank back or the inside of a cover, drawn as slabs with nothing to click. Flipping moves through pages one at a time, so the spread changes when the open page crosses into the next pair.

**Sections.** Sections are the notebook's order: `pagesInOrder(sections, pages)` sorts by section order then `createdAt`, and everything that walks pages (the rail, previous and next, numbering, search results, the PDF) walks that order. The side sequence is a pure function of the sections and the ordered pages (`sideSequence` in `src/page/sides.ts`): inside front cover; for each section its divider front, its divider back, its pages, and a blank back when the section ends on a right-hand side; the inside back cover last. Side kinds: `cover`, `divider` (front or back, with the section), `page`, `blank`. A page's number is its 1-based position among the page sides; its side is the parity of its index in the sequence. The rail lays its columns out from the same order, one per section, with the section's `lastPageId` behind the tab. The one-page rule and every page operation are unchanged; only "which page comes next" and "which side is this" read the sections. Backup version 9 and Dexie version 10 (after Phase 6's 8 and 9): `tags` become `sections` and `tagId` becomes `sectionId`. Conversion: a first section takes the untagged pages (named "Notes", coloured like the cover), then one section per tag in the tags' order with the tag's name and colour; every section starts with `lastPageId` null. This is the one time pages reorder: pages that were tagged move to their section's run. Nothing is lost.

**One page at a time.** A single predicate, `canAddPage` (`src/notebook/pageRules.ts`), says whether a new page may be added: not while the open page is empty (a lined page with blank columns, a zine page with no rows), by the store's own notion of empty, the one the kind change uses. Every entry point asks it: the page bar's add and the shortcuts; and the session's `newPage` refuses on it too, so no path around the controls exists. It lives in one module so the rule can be relaxed or removed in one place.

**Search.** Pages are plain strings. For drawings, walk the page's text elements (they carry `originalText`).

**PDF.** Render each page to PNG at 2x (off screen, at zoom 1, in preview mode, through the same renderer as thumbnails) and place it on a PDF page of the matching physical size (A5 148x210mm, A4 210x297mm, Letter 8.5x11in), respecting orientation, with pdf-lib.

**Multiple notebooks.** Notebook metadata is one record; pages, canvas and files are their own records under the notebook id. The shelf reads only metadata. A notebook is loaded fully when opened.

## 7. Decisions log

- No fork of Excalidraw. Embed the npm package and control it from outside.
- Pages never reorder. Date order is the only order.
- One tag per page.
- Page size and orientation are notebook-level.
- Date stamp and page number are overlays, not canvas elements.
- Browser storage first, backups as downloadable files. No accounts, no backend.
- 2026-09-17: Pages are text only, written in our own editor on lined paper. Drawing lives on one infinite Excalidraw canvas per notebook. This replaces "every page is an Excalidraw canvas" and the paper snapping spec that followed it. Reason: Excalidraw has no document flow and no public way to start editing an element, so a note editor built on it would fight it forever, while its grid mode already gives live snapping for drawings.
- Blank, dotted and grid paper are gone. Pages are lined; the canvas has a grid toggle; "clear" is a view mode.
- One canvas per notebook. Pages remember a canvas view instead of owning canvases.
- A full page stops accepting input. The user creates the next page.
- Plain text only on pages in Phase 1. Basic formatting (bold, italic, colour, alignment) is in scope for Phase 2 and moves the page editor onto ProseMirror; columns stay plain strings until then.
- Text formatting (2026-09-17): the page editor is ProseMirror with the schema of section 5. The text colour palette is Excalidraw's five default stroke picks, so pages and the canvas share one set of colours; the black swatch means the page's ink and is stored as no mark. Alignment shortcuts follow Google Docs (Cmd+Shift+L / E / R). Bold and italic are synthetic, since Excalifont has one weight; a bolder handwriting face is not planned. Colours outside the palette are dropped on paste. Columns are `{ text, doc }`, with `text` derived from `doc`; the backup format and the IndexedDB schema are at version 2, and version 1 data is converted with one paragraph per old line.
- App bar controls are icon buttons with hover tooltips (label plus shortcut). Page navigation sits at the left of the bar; page settings, the notebook menu, preview and the panel toggle at the right.
- "Clear view" is called Preview.
- "Two columns" lives in page settings, with the page's metadata.
- The page has a thin border and rounded corners and no shadow, like the canvas panel.
- Visual and branding refinement is deferred to the end; the control layout is settled when the rail arrives, and the paper look before export.
- Notebook cover added to scope (2026-09-17): colour, optional emoji or initial, and subtitle, stored on the notebook and included in backups. Data and settings in Phase 2, the shelf card in Phase 3. Its exact look is part of the visual pass at the end.
- The divider snaps to 10mm steps and defaults to the midpoint of the writable area. The margin line offset is a notebook default, adjustable per page.
- The caret is drawn by the shell, centred between rules, because a native text caret spans the whole line box and straddles the rule. Known issue: in the user's Chrome the caret still appears to cross the rule below it, while the built-in browser shows it centred; to be investigated in that Chrome directly.
- The left rail sits under the app bar, to the left of the desk, with its first square aligned to the top of the page. The list scrolls on its own and keeps the open page's square in view; the hover label (and later the hover preview) floats beside the rail rather than inside the scrolling list, so it is never clipped.

- Clippings (2026-09-18): the canvas is where drawings are made; a clipping is how a drawing comes onto a page, as a free object that overlaps the text without affecting it. This is deliberately not the float approach noted on 2026-09-17 (text wrapping around images): text and clippings are two layers that ignore each other, and it is the user who leaves gaps and plays with the overlap. The in-app "Clip to page" is the primary path (Excalidraw's `exportToSvg` on the selection, no clipboard permission, a vector result that scales crisply) and the clipboard paste (Excalidraw's Copy as SVG or PNG, or any image) is the second, so clippings can also come from elsewhere. Clippings are files in the notebook's files table, shared with the media pool and the canvas, so one storage, one backup shape, one deletion rule cover them; they show in the pool. Stored in mm with a width only, since the height follows the image. Two layers rather than a z-order among clippings: "over" and "under" the text; clippings among themselves paint in creation order. A clipping under the text is reached with Alt (Option) held, the modifier the app already owns. Dark paper inverts clippings the way Excalidraw's dark mode inverts its canvas, accepting that a photo inside a clipping inverts too, since clippings are drawings. Lined pages only for now; zine pages and rotation are on the Later list. Backup version 8 and Dexie version 9, an additive field.
- Sections (2026-09-18): tags become sections, the physical divisions of a notebook, so one commonplace notebook can hold a journal, work notes and reflections and open at the right one in a click. Every page is in a section and every notebook has at least one; sections are ordered and order the pages (section order, then creation order), so page numbers run through the notebook by section as in a bound book, and this replaces "never reordered" with "never reordered by hand": the one reordering happens when the converter groups tagged pages into their sections. The rail is one column per section with a tab; the tag filter goes, since the columns are the filter. Each section remembers where it was left (a stored `lastPageId`, like the notebook's), so its tab is a bookmark. In the spread, each section begins with a divider leaf in its colour, its name on the front, so the first page of a section is always a right-hand page (a blank back is inserted after a section that ends on a right-hand page), which is what a real divider does; dividers and blanks are unnumbered and not clickable, and they stay out of the PDF until the spread print exists. The first section of a converted notebook is "Notes" in the cover's colour and takes the untagged pages, then the tags follow in their order. Deleting a section moves its pages to a neighbour after a confirm; the last section cannot be deleted. Backup version 9 and Dexie version 10, after Phase 6's numbers. "Tags" was always the wrong name for what these are; the rename happens with this change rather than later.
- Zine pages behind a feature flag (2026-09-18): the first release may ship with lined pages and the canvas only, zine pages following. The gate is the narrowest one that works: it hides the ways a zine page is created and the pool's peek, and nothing else reads it, so the model, the store, the converters, search, exports and backups stay exactly as they are and a zine page that already exists keeps working, the pool included. A build-time variable with a local-storage override rather than a setting in the app, since the user is not meant to see a switch. The flag is temporary and is listed in section 8 so it is removed knowingly.
- Drawing on the page (2026-09-18): the side canvas was the answer to "Excalidraw is the wrong tool for text on lines"; the better answer is to keep our editor for the text and put Excalidraw on the page only for drawing, which the Phase 0 spike had already shown works (page-sized, zoom locked, clipped, toolbar stripped). So the infinite canvas per notebook and its side panel leave the UI, every page carries its own drawing in page coordinates, and drawing is a mode of the page: the writing is locked and can be dimmed or hidden while drawing, and the drawing is a still picture while writing. This replaces clippings (a copy of a drawing on the page) with the drawing itself, and makes the highlight mark redundant (a translucent stroke is a highlighter); the mark stays for now since it has landed, its removal is on the Later list. The one-canvas-per-notebook idea, the per-page canvas views and the canvas PNG export go dormant rather than being deleted: the data stays in storage and backups, nothing is moved onto pages by itself, and the code is listed in section 8. Drawing mode is for one page at a time; the other page of a spread shows its still. The viewing aids (text and rules opacity) are per browser, never part of the page, so exports always show everything. The panel remains for the media pool on zine pages only. Phase 8 goes before Phase 7's sections; the Phase 6 clippings work stops.
- Zine feature flag, built (2026-09-18): `src/features.ts` exports one `features` object read at module load: `zinePages` is the local-storage override (`typestill.features.zine` holding `on` or `off`) when there is one, else on unless `VITE_FEATURE_ZINE` is `off`; `.env.production` sets it off, so `pnpm build` ships lined-only and `pnpm dev` has zine pages. App reads the flag and passes it down: the page bar's New page control becomes a plain `IconButton` ("New page", ⌥N, the same one-page hint when off) instead of the lined-or-zine menu, `PageSettings` takes a `showKind` prop and drops the Kind row, and the panel's peek control is not rendered. The ⌥⇧N chord stays claimed by the shell and does nothing, rather than falling through to the browser (Option+Shift+N would type a character into the page editor). Nothing else reads the flag.
- Drawing on the page, built (2026-09-18): every page carries `drawing` (Excalidraw elements in page coordinates, deleted ones dropped) and `drawingLayer`; backup version 8 and Dexie version 9 give older pages an empty drawing over the text. Drawing mode is App state kept in the tab's session storage (`typestill.drawing.mode`), switched by the page bar's pencil or Shift+Tab (⌥D at first, see the feedback entry), and it follows the open page: flipping pages in drawing mode remounts the editor on the new page. The editor (`src/page/PageDrawing.tsx`, from the Phase 0 spike) covers the desk above the sheet (z-index 5; the page bar sits at 6 so its controls and popovers stay reachable), pinned to the open page's box computed from the desk's size and the fit, one border in, since the page's content is laid out inside its 1px border; the page-sized still is drawn from the same origin at the page's outer size, so a scene px is the same length live and still. Locks as in the spike: capture-phase wheel (pinch included), keyboard zoom and pan chords and middle-button swallowed, the hand tool and the grid reset from `onChange`, any reported scroll or zoom set back, the canvases clipped to the page. The editor's `theme` follows the paper, not the app appearance (dark paper gets Excalidraw's dark theme), so what is drawn looks the way the still will, which takes the same inversion filter on dark paper; the editor's chrome therefore matches the paper rather than the chrome. Change reports are throttled to one per 200ms while a stroke is drawn; the session's `setDrawing(pageId, content, loadId)` saves through a per-page saver keyed by element versions (an editor reporting its last state for a page just left saves straight away, and a page deleted this session is ignored) and puts the live elements in state, where the still (`src/page/stills.ts`, `exportToSvg` over the elements plus a transparent page frame as `exportingFrame`, cached by element versions, 64 entries) reads them; the frame is pinned to (0, 0) after `convertToExcalidrawElements`, which pads a frame by 10px and would otherwise shift the whole still down and to the right. Files pasted images use go into the notebook's files table with the save and into the session's files, so the pool lists them with a page badge. The still is an `<img>` before the columns in the page's DOM, z-index 2 over or 0 under, which the renderer keeps, so thumbnails, PDF and PNG carry it; exports prime the stills before mounting. Thumbnails are not rendered in drawing mode and refresh when it ends. The viewing aids (`src/page/drawingMode.ts`: text and rules at full, dimmed 0.3 or hidden, in local storage) are custom properties on the page under `.is-drawing`, where the columns also take no pointer events; the drawing tools popover in the page bar holds them and the page's layer. A click on the other page of the spread, through the editor's container, opens it. The side panel is now the media pool only, opened from a page bar button on zine pages, closed by a page change or by entering drawing mode; its minimum width dropped from Excalidraw's 742px to 332px and the main column's minimum rose to 800px so the desk stays wider than Excalidraw's mobile breakpoint. Search walks each page's text then its drawn text (`originalText`), one result per page marked "· drawing". Removed from the UI: the canvas panel and its toggle, ⌥\, "Export canvas as PNG" and the pool's peek; the code that stays is listed in section 8.
- Sections, built (2026-09-18): `Notebook.sections` (ordered `Section { id, name, color, lastPageId }`) and `Page.sectionId` replace tags; backup version 9 and Dexie version 10 convert through one function, `sectionsFromTags` in `src/notebook/sections.ts`: a first "Notes" section in the cover's colour takes the untagged pages, then one section per tag in the tags' order keeping the tag's id, so nothing else is remapped; a section's stale `lastPageId` reads as null. The order of pages is `pagesInOrder(sections, pages)` and the session's `pages` is always in it, so the rail, previous and next, numbering, search results, thumbnails and the PDF follow it with no change of their own; the store's `deletePage` picks the neighbour in that order and the replacement for an only page keeps its section. A new notebook has one section, Notes; `createPage` takes a section (the open page's, from the session's `newPage`; an empty section's tab creates its first page there, under the one-page rule, and does nothing otherwise). Moving a page to another section (page settings' Section select, or "New section…", a prompt) re-sorts and the page keeps its identity at a new index; so does moving a section up or down. Deleting a section moves its pages to the section before it (after it, for the first) after a confirm naming the count and the destination; the last section's delete button is disabled. Each section remembers the page last opened in it (`setSectionLastPage`, written together with the notebook's `lastPageId` whenever a page is opened; the store clears it when that page is deleted), and its tab in the rail opens it, else the section's last page. The rail is one column per section in order, each a tab in the section's colour (its name on hover) over the squares in the section's colour, the open page's ringed, with the hover label "Page 7 · Work" and the thumbnail; the filter is gone. Notebook settings' Tags group became Sections (name, the cover palette, ↑ ↓, Delete, Add section) and its edits apply at once.
- Divider leaves, built (2026-09-18): `sideSequence(sections, orderedPages)` in `src/page/sides.ts` (pure, tested) is the notebook as sides: the inside of the front cover (side 0, a left-hand side); for each section in order its divider front and back and its pages, then a blank back when the section ended on a right-hand side; the inside of the back cover last, always a right-hand side. `sideAt` is the parity, `pageSide` and `sideIndexOfPage` find a page, and `spreadOf(sides, index)` is the pair the side belongs to, both sides always present. App builds the sequence each render (it is a few hundred entries at most) and the spread shows the open page beside whatever side faces it: a page (read-only until clicked), a divider (a slab in the section's colour, its name in white on the front), a blank back (a slab in the theme's paper colour with the page's border) or the inside of a cover (the cover colour); slabs are page-sized, round their outer corners and take no pointer events. In drawing mode, a click through the editor on the other side opens it only when it is a page. The rail rounds thumbnails from the same sequence. The first spread, (inside cover | first divider), holds no page and so is never shown. The PDF stays pages only.
- Feedback on the MVP branch (2026-09-18): the rail's squares are a light tint of the section colour (`color-mix` of the colour at 35% over the desk, 60% on hover) with the open page's square in the full colour, in place of the ring; the inside of a cover and the back of a divider are plain surfaces in the chrome's colours (`--surface` with a `--line` border: white in the light appearance, dark grey in the dark one) rather than the cover or section colour, since a dark slab beside page 1 read as a wall, while the divider's front keeps the section colour and name and a blank back stays paper; the page of a spread that is not open is dimmed to 55% (from 85%) so the open one is obvious; and drawing mode's shortcut is Shift+Tab, the one chord without Alt, because it is the shortcut used most and plain Tab stays the browser's for moving focus (native fields keep both). The tooltip shows "⇧⇥" on a Mac and "Shift+Tab" elsewhere.
- The owner's look at the MVP branch (2026-09-18, Phase 9): the page number leaves the page, since the page bar already says where you are, and numbering becomes something an export may do one day. The page bar's navigation is centred on the page with the other controls at its ends, so the eye finds previous and next where the page is. Notebook and page settings stop being two places: one notebook box holds the map (the sections grid that was the rail, now a view of the whole notebook, like zooming out), the sections, the little that is per page (section, layout, kind behind the flag, created) and the notebook's groups, applying changes as they are made; the rail leaves the desk because the map serves the same purpose in a place that can be wide enough to preview pages, and the counter and ⌥M make it one click away. The margin line becomes a notebook setting every page follows: a bound notebook has one margin, and per-page adjustment was more setting than it was worth. Drawing loses the over-or-under choice, since Excalidraw's per-element opacity already lets a drawing sit back, and the dimming controls are named for what they are. Divider backs carry the section as a tab on paper rather than a full-colour slab: the first spread of a section then reads as the section opening, which is what the owner wanted, without the wall the earlier full-colour back had been. Every field that leaves the UI stays stored and listed in section 8; there is no format change in this phase. The media pool opens a new list, section 9, of features that may be descoped before release.

## 8. In the code, not in the UI

Things that exist in the code without being reachable from the interface, kept so they can come back or be cleaned up knowingly. Add to this list whenever something is descoped from the UI but left in the code; remove from it when the code goes or the feature returns.

- Multiple notebooks (descoped from the UI 2026-09-18, Phase 5): the shelf screen (`src/notebook/Shelf.tsx`, its CSS and test; App still renders it, but only as the session's fallback when the last notebook fails to load at start-up), the notebook switcher (`src/notebook/NotebookSwitcher.tsx` and its CSS, imported by nothing), the session's `Shelf` state with `closeNotebook`, `openNotebook`, `createNotebook` (outside the first run), `renameNotebook` (from the shelf; the settings dialog owns renaming now) and `deleteNotebook`, the session's `notebooks` listing, and the store's `renameNotebook` (the settings dialog renames through `updateNotebookSettings`); the store's `listNotebooks` and `deleteNotebook` are in use, by the start-up load and the backup replace flow. The storage model stays multi-notebook by design (section 3), so sync and a future shelf need no format change. The first-run welcome dialog and the backup replace flow are the only ways a notebook is created.
- The zine stored shape is more general than the page allows (section 3): rows may hold any blocks, two per row; the app allows one media block, one text beside it and one text row (`zineOptions`). Relaxing the rule is a one-place change.
- Fonts from a file in the notebook (`Font.source: "file"`, the FontFace path noted in section 5) for custom themes, which are on the Later list; only bundled fonts are used.
- The infinite canvas (superseded by drawing on the page, Phase 8, built 2026-09-18): `src/canvas/Canvas.tsx` and its CSS (imported by nothing; the `CanvasContent` shape is what the session's dormant canvas autosave still takes), the `SidePanel` icon, `exportCanvasPng` in `src/notebook/export.tsx` and the `canvas` kind of `exportFileName`, the store's canvas record with `getCanvas`, `setCanvasGrid` and `saveCanvasContent`, the per-page `canvasView` (still accepted by `updatePage`), and in the session the canvas autosave and view saver, `onCanvasChange`, `onCanvasViewChange`, `onGridChange` and `restoreView`; the session's `canvas` and `canvasFileIds` are still read, by the storage figures and the pool's usage index, so the canvas's images keep their badge and are never pruned. The canvas mode of the side panel, its toggle and the ⌥\ shortcut, "Export canvas as PNG" and the search's `scrollTo` handle were removed outright. The record stays in storage and in backups so nothing drawn there is lost. Remove knowingly, with a format bump, if the canvas never returns.
- Page numbers (off the page since Phase 9): `src/page/PageMarks.tsx` and its CSS, the `showPageNumber` field on pages and in the notebook defaults, the store's `setPageMarks` path. Kept for an export option that numbers pages; remove with a format bump if that never comes.
- The per-page margin line (Phase 9): `Page.margin` is stored and validated but every page renders the notebook's margin setting; the store's `setPageMargin` and the divider re-snap on margin change stay. Remove with a format bump if a per-page margin never returns.
- The drawing layer (Phase 9): `Page.drawingLayer` is stored, always "over" for new pages, and the still always paints over the text; the "under" branch of `DrawingStill` and the control in the drawing tools are gone from the UI. Remove with a format bump if it never returns.
- Zine pages when the `zinePages` feature flag is off (Phase 7, built 2026-09-18): the whole zine page kind, the zine entry of the page bar's New page menu, the Kind row of page settings and the ⌥⇧N shortcut are in the code and in every build, hidden by `src/features.ts` (App reads it and passes props down) so the app can ship lined-only first; the panel's peek control it also hid left with the canvas panel. Existing zine pages still open, with the pool. Remove the flag, and this line, when zine pages ship.

## 9. Candidates for descoping

Features kept in the UI for now that the owner may take out of the first release. A line here is a question, not a decision: deciding moves the feature to section 8 (kept in the code, hidden) or removes it, or takes it off this list.

- The media pool (2026-09-18): with zine pages behind their flag, the pool is reachable only on zine pages, so a lined-only release ships without it already; the question is whether it stays at all once zine pages ship, or whether images on zine pages come only from paste, drop and the file picker with no library to browse. Drawings take pasted images without it.
